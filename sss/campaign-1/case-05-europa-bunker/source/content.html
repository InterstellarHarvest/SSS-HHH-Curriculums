Mission question

What best explains the repeated seedling failure, and what problem must engineers define before long-duration crop cultivation continues?

Case briefing

Wheat seeds germinate and begin growing. Brown spots then appear, growing tips become abnormal, and each planting fails. Use several independent evidence sources; one symptom is not enough.

Reasoning rule

Exposure is not identical to biological damage. Biological evidence can be consistent with DNA damage without proving an exact molecular mechanism.

Teacher Guide

Lesson overview

Students investigate the repeated crop failure. They distinguish observations from interpretations, converge four formal clue routes, compare diagnoses, build a qualitative process model, write CER, and define a crop-protection design problem.

Correct diagnosis

Radiation is the best-supported diagnosis. Elevated ionizing-radiation evidence, meristem abnormalities consistent with DNA damage, repeated failure, and an incomplete crop assessment converge.

Duration

One 60-minute lesson; gameplay target 15–20 minutes.

Student product

Eight-task packet ending with a precise design-problem definition and qualified recommendation.

Collect

Student pages 1–4. Score all eight tasks; prioritize Tasks 3–8 for evidence-based reasoning.

Suggested sequence

7–25Game or fallbackStudents collect all four formal clues.

Materials and technology

Case 05 game access or Student evidence digest; printed or digital packet; pencil. Test the central editor and game locally. No radiation calculation, external reading, or artwork is required.

Teacher Guide · Outcomes and standards
SolarAgriculturalAgency

Measurable objectives

  • Classify case statements as observations or interpretations.
  • Explain how four independent sources converge without claiming any one clue proves the cause.
  • Compare radiation with gravity, minerals, and light using supplied evidence.
  • Construct a qualitative process model that preserves uncertainty.
  • Define a crop-protection design problem with criteria, constraints, scientific principles, and verification evidence.

Success criteria

  • Uses evidence from at least three sources in CER.
  • Distinguishes exposure, biological evidence, and diagnosis.
  • States that ionizing radiation can affect dividing and nondividing cells while explaining why meristem damage is especially disruptive.
  • Includes at least two criteria and three relevant constraints in the engineering definition.
  • Separates immediate operations from durable engineering and proposes a testable verification plan.

Standards alignment and limitation

Direct assessment: MS-ETS1-1

Tasks 7–8 require students to define a solvable crop-protection problem with multiple criteria and constraints, relevant radiation and plant-growth principles, and evidence needed to verify success.

Supporting alignment: MS-ETS1-2

Students discuss responses, but no controlled comparison source provides competing solutions evaluated against common criteria and constraints. Do not claim direct assessment of MS-ETS1-2.

SEP

Asking Questions and Defining Problems; Engaging in Argument from Evidence; Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions.

CCC / DCI

Cause and Effect; Systems and System Models; ETS1.A with radiation biology as supporting science content.

Academic vocabulary

Constraint, criterion, ionizing radiation, meristem, modeled secondary radiation. Student and Accessible vocabulary are alphabetized and use the same displayed terms.

Prerequisite knowledge

Students should know that cells contain DNA, roots and shoots grow from specialized regions, evidence can support several explanations, and engineering criteria differ from constraints.

Teacher Guide · Preparation and facilitation
SolarAgriculturalAgency

Preparation

  1. Open Campaign 1 Case 05 and confirm the four clue routes.
  2. Prepare the evidence digest as the no-game fallback.
  3. Review the qualitative process model and prohibited claims.
  4. Decide whether students type or handwrite; preserve individual completion.

Facilitation guidance

Launch

Ask what students can observe before asking what caused the failure. Do not reveal radiation.

Game

Direct students to crew start → problem_main, sensors start → radiation_detail, plants start → microscope, and logs start → construction_log.

Evidence

During 3 · Converge the Four Clue Routes, ask what each source contributes and what it cannot establish alone.

Model

During 5 · Model the Radiation-to-Growth Pathway, require the exposure-versus-damage distinction.

Engineering

During 7 · Define the Engineering Design Problem, reject invented thresholds and require criteria that can be verified qualitatively or through later approved testing.

Checks for understanding

  • “Is this statement an observation, an interpretation, or a conclusion?”
  • “Which source establishes exposure? Which provides biological evidence?”
  • “Why is the construction log still necessary?”
  • “What would make a criterion testable without inventing a number?”

Accessibility and differentiation

Offer the Accessible edition, read one evidence card at a time, allow speech-to-text, and let students point to evidence labels before writing. Keep the same eight tasks and scoring targets. Extension: identify one uncertainty that future crop testing must resolve.

Science boundaries

Established science

Ionizing radiation can damage DNA in dividing and nondividing cells. Meristems contain actively dividing cells needed for continuing root and shoot growth, so damage there can be especially disruptive.

Europa context

Energetic particles are trapped and accelerated in Jupiter’s magnetosphere. Europa lies within this particle environment.

Modeled extrapolation

The fictional bunker’s internal exposure and material-generated secondary radiation are qualitative modeled scenario evidence, not a validated transport or plant-dosimetry result.

Evidence limit

Scattered necrotic spots are observations. Their distribution alone cannot exclude every nutrient deficiency or pathogen attack.

Mechanism language

Preferred: “The abnormalities are consistent with DNA damage, and the converging evidence makes radiation the best-supported diagnosis.” Avoid “the spots prove radiation,” “radiation only harms dividing cells,” or claims of exact strand-break counts.

Scenario authority

Use the corrected game wording. Crew and crop risk require separate assessment, and the radiation monitor’s elevated status is qualitative.

Misconceptions and corrective prompts
MisconceptionTeacher response

Tempting alternatives

  • Gravity: roots form before failure, so gravity does not fit the observed meristem damage and elevated-radiation evidence.
  • Minerals: water and nutrient readings are nominal; no chemical-contamination pattern is supplied.
  • Light: the standard lighting setup does not explain the radiation monitor, plant microscopy, or incomplete crop assessment.

Technical fallback

If gameplay is unavailable, students use the complete text-first evidence digest in Task 3. Read the four source rows in order. The fallback supplies every clue needed for diagnosis, modeling, CER, and engineering problem definition.

Individual completion

Discussion is optional. Each student submits an individual packet. Game speed, score, rank, rapport, optional nodes, and bonus discoveries are excluded from academic grading.

Annotated answers and grading guidance

1 · Frame the Mission Problem

Look for repeated seedling failure after germination and the need for crop-specific exposure/risk evidence. Do not require a proposed fix yet.

2 · Separate Observation from Interpretation

Monitor status and cell appearance are observations; radiation as best-supported diagnosis is an interpretation/conclusion. Credit explanations that observations can be checked directly while interpretations connect evidence.

3 · Converge the Four Clue Routes

Each row should state a distinct contribution: persistence, exposure, biological damage evidence, and unverified crop protection. Strong responses also name a limit.

4 · Compare the Competing Diagnoses

Radiation fits all four sources. Gravity, minerals, and light fail to account for the full pattern. Do not award full credit for selecting radiation without comparison.

Teacher Guide · Annotated guidance II
SolarAgriculturalAgency
5 · Model the Radiation-to-Growth Pathway

Sequence material interactions, monitor evidence, exposure limit, meristem abnormalities, growth disruption, and convergence. Full credit requires the explicit exposure-versus-damage distinction.

6 · Explain the Diagnosis with CER

Claim: radiation is best-supported. Evidence: at least three sources. Reasoning: connects exposure and biological evidence while preserving uncertainty about exact mechanism and modeled secondary contribution.

7 · Define the Engineering Design Problem

Need: enable crop cultivation with verified crop protection. Criteria may include reducing grow-chamber exposure, supporting normal plant development, and producing monitoring evidence. Constraints include Europa’s particle environment, material interactions, separate crew/crop assessment, limited habitat resources, and unknown crop criteria.

8 · Recommend Immediate and Durable Responses

Immediate responses should reduce or pause crop exposure while preserving evidence and operations. Durable responses may revise shielding, chamber placement, monitoring, or crop-specific verification. Credit other defensible responses; none is presented as already validated.

Answer Key · Engineering definition
SolarAgriculturalAgency

Need: Define a crop-protection system that permits long-duration cultivation while producing evidence that crop exposure and biological response are adequately controlled.

Criteria: Reduce grow-chamber ionizing-radiation exposure; support normal meristem development and repeated crop growth; provide monitoring that can verify crop performance.

Constraints: Europa’s trapped energetic-particle environment; possible secondary radiation from material interactions; limited habitat mass, volume, power, and placement; separate crew and crop assessments; crop-specific protection criteria are not yet verified.

Verification: Compare monitor trends, plant microscopy, growth-stage outcomes, and repeated crop trials under the revised system. Do not substitute a crew criterion or invent a crop threshold.

Immediate operational response: Pause or limit long-duration planting, preserve monitoring data and plant samples, and move cultivation to the best-protected available location if operations can do so without claiming safety is verified.

Durable engineering response: Redesign or supplement grow-chamber protection, placement, and monitoring, then conduct a crop-specific validation program that considers both primary and modeled secondary radiation.

Justification and verification: The response follows from converging exposure, plant, repeated-failure, and construction-log evidence. Success requires improved monitor evidence together with normal meristem development and sustained crop growth across repeated trials.

Acceptable variation: Other cautious operational and durable engineering responses may earn full credit when they meet the stated criteria, respect the constraints, and include a verification plan. The packet does not establish that one proposed design is already successful.
