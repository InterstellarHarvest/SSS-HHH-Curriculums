# Teacher Case Analysis — ISS Greenhouse Module

## Story problem

An ISS lettuce crop is alive but stunted. Roots curl into tangled balls instead of extending through the growth medium, and stems grow at irregular angles.

## Scientific phenomenon

Plants use multiple directional responses. Gravitropism normally provides a consistent orientation cue: roots generally grow with gravity while shoots grow against it. In microgravity, gravity-sensing structures do not settle in one stable direction, so other cues become more important.

## Evidence map

| Source | Essential evidence | Instructional role |
|---|---|---|
| Crew | Roots do not consistently grow downward; changing resources did not solve the pattern. | Establishes the symptom and failed interventions. |
| Sensors | Microgravity is the major environmental difference; nutrients, water, temperature, humidity, and lighting are nominal. | Identifies the strongest causal difference and rejects resource explanations. |
| Plants | Dense root tangles, poor anchoring, and unusual stem angles. | Provides direct biological observations. |
| Logs | Defines the gravitropism/statolith mechanism and suggests replacement cues. | Supplies mechanism and engineering possibilities. |

## Essential reasoning path

1. Root tangling is the observed symptom.
2. Normal nutrient, water, and light readings weaken common resource explanations.
3. Microgravity is the major unusual environmental condition.
4. Mission logs connect gravity sensing to directional growth.
5. Disrupted gravitropism best accounts for the evidence.
6. A useful engineering response supplies another consistent cue rather than merely adding resources.

## Optional evidence

- Similar root anomalies occurred during prior missions.
- Plant pillows distribute water from multiple directions, which can reduce a moisture gradient.
- Directional lighting can help shoots but is less direct for roots.
- Physical root guides can provide a touch or structure cue.

## Distractors

| Distractor | Why tempting | Why rejected |
|---|---|---|
| Nutrient burn | Plants are pale and stunted. | Nutrient readings are nominal; directional tangling is a poor match. |
| Wrong light cycle | Stems are angled and leaves are pale. | The 16/8 cycle and light levels are nominal; roots are the strongest abnormality. |
| Defective seeds | All plants show similar symptoms. | The pattern repeats with the environment and prior plantings. |
| Water problem | Roots respond to water. | Delivery works; uniform wicking may reduce one cue but does not best explain the case. |

The nutrient, light, and seed distractors are the three incorrect options on the in-game Diagnose screen. Water is an additional Teacher discussion distractor, not a selectable game diagnosis.

## Instructional identity

**Student role:** Pattern investigator.  
**Primary practice:** Compare multiple evidence types, eliminate alternatives, explain a mechanism, and define an engineering problem.

## Boundary notes

The game simplifies a complex plant response. Real plant responses in microgravity involve interacting genetic, hormonal, mechanical, fluid, and environmental effects. Do not teach that roots are completely random, that statoliths are the only gravity-sensing mechanism, or that plant pillows fully restore orientation.
