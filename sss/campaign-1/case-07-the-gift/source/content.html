Teacher Guide

Lesson overview

This one-period Campaign 1 culmination asks students to combine four essential evidence channels, model a missing source–signal–receptor interaction, reject plausible alternatives, and compare three fictional interventions against common criteria and constraints. The game may motivate evidence collection, but every fact required for assessment appears in the packet.

Learning target

I can combine evidence from several sources to explain a missing biological trigger and justify a contained intervention.

Success criteria

Students distinguish matched from complete, establish viability, connect source to receptor response, reject three distractors, and state safety controls and uncertainty.

Measurable objectives

  1. Compare the 12 trace identifiers in the isolated lab with the 847+ near living growth and explain why different sets do not identify the active cue.
  2. Use at least three of four essential clues to build a six-link causal explanation.
  3. Reject light, transfer damage/death, and human microbes using supplied evidence.
  4. Compare all three interventions using evidence fit, contamination, verification, reversibility, authorization, and uncertainty.
  5. Produce an independent CER and exit response that state a limitation.

Launch / gameplay / post-game flow

Launch

Accept multiple missing-variable hypotheses. Insist that “matched measured targets” is not “all biological conditions.”

Gameplay

Return from story outcomes to evidence status: predicted/narrated responses are not replicated trials.

Acceptable answer: Integrity, primed chambers, and selective membrane response support a viable dormant state. They conflict with death and with transfer destruction. They do not show that active germination has started, identify the ligand, establish a safe dose, or prove the mature source.

Likely misconceptions and responses

“99.7% means everything important matches.”

It is a fictional composite of primary measured targets; trace compounds are excluded and no weighting formula exists.

“12 of 847 proves which molecule matters.”

It identifies a broad trace-context gap. Receptor and archive evidence are needed to narrow it.

“Responsive means germinating.”

A response supports viability and a receptor gate. It does not show commitment or chamber opening.

“Near means a mother organism feeds offspring.”

Proximity supports source and transport only. Do not infer intention, kin preference, or nutrient transfer.

“Molecular formula tells us how to synthesize it.”

Formula does not specify connectivity, stereochemistry, carrier, purity, biological activity, dose, or safety.

“The best diagnosis is certain.”

Best-supported means it explains the converging evidence with fewer conflicts; it still carries fictional-model uncertainty.

Check for understanding: Ask students to name one observation needed to revive each rejected diagnosis. This keeps distractors plausible as hypotheses while separating them from conclusions supported by the current record.

Intervention caution: No safe numerical dose appears in the evidence. Do not accept an invented concentration. Scores +10, +5, and +0 communicate relative story evidence; they are neither scientific results nor student grades.

Monitoring sequence and stopping rule

  1. Before: verify containment integrity, source identity, cell exclusion, baseline receptor activity, and two-party authorization.
  2. During pre-commitment: monitor cue identity/concentration categorically, leakage, contamination, receptor activity, and specimen integrity. Stop for unexpected material, loss of containment, adverse response, or failed verification.
  3. After commitment: monitor membrane change, network-precursor activation, chamber opening, canopy/root development, and stabilization. Do not claim reversibility after commitment.

Predicted response and outcome status

One cycle is about 6 Earth hours. Authored routes predict first glow/receptor response after 2, 3, or 4 cycles (12, 18, or 24 h), then 4–6 cycles (24–36 h) of post-commitment stabilization. These are modeled/narrated outcomes, not replicated tests. A lack of verified receptor change within the predicted window, a response without the target cue, or an unexpected adverse response would weaken the model.

Common decision criteria and constraints

Criteria

Evidence fit, cue authenticity, controlled delivery, measurable response, minimal manipulation.

Constraints

Containment, contamination control, verification, uncertainty, reversibility before commitment, time, and joint SAA–Zhel'ii authorization.

Case 06 and Case 07 both require looking beyond primary environmental targets to a signaling interaction. In Case 06, filtering removed compounds from a system that had been coordinating normally. In Case 07, the isolated lab recreated known primary conditions but never included the mature biological source or shared path. Checking the source, transported cue, receptor, and response produces a stronger explanation because it identifies the different missing link in each case instead of treating one environmental reading as the whole system.

Independent exit response — completed exemplar

Pod integrity, intact primed chambers, dense receptors, and selective membrane responses show that the pod is viable and waiting rather than dead. Those observations are not enough to identify the missing trigger because many ligands or conditions could produce a receptor-gated state; the trace comparison, archive mechanism, and mature-source/proximity evidence are needed to identify the specific missing cue.

Acceptable answer guidance

  • Students may compare Case 07 with a teacher-supplied earlier case if the comparison accurately names a shift from isolated variables to interacting system parts.
  • Strong answers distinguish necessary evidence from sufficient evidence: viability is necessary for the dormant diagnosis but insufficient to identify the trigger.
  • Accept bullets or sentences. Require an independently stated explanation, not a copied phrase bank.
  • Do not award or withhold credit based on game score, intervention score, route, rank, speed, or optional dialogue.
Assessment close: A complete response names both what the evidence supports and what remains uncertain. It does not declare the campaign instructional sequence complete or infer a future curriculum package.
