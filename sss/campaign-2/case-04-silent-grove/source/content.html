Student Mission
Mission question

Every grove reading is nominal and the three organisms are healthy, so why has the network’s signalling stopped — and what would a corrected light schedule have to specify?

Case briefing

The Thal-Oren is a Zhel’ii ship carrying a grove of three intertwined organisms that coordinate by releasing volatile compounds into the air. For two years the grove signalled on a stable daily cycle. After a power fluctuation, the caretakers changed the grove lights from 18 hours on and 6 hours off to continuous illumination. Three months later the grove is structurally healthy and no signal can be measured at all.

Reasoning rule

Day numbers count backwards from today, so Day −90 is ninety days ago. A reading of 0.0 ppb is a measurement at the instrument’s reporting threshold, not a count of zero molecules. Copy every reported value exactly, keep 40–80 ppb as a range, and do not write a value for any hour or day the records do not report.

Teacher Guide

Lesson overview

Students investigate why a shipboard grove of three intertwined organisms has stopped producing the volatile compounds it uses to coordinate. Every routine reading is nominal, the organisms are structurally healthy, and the only condition that changed was the light schedule: caretakers replaced 18 hours on and 6 hours off with continuous illumination. The investigation separates how much light from when the light stops, and ends with a written schedule specification and a monitored trial.

The packet is built for grades 6–8 and adapts upward for grades 9–12 and adult learners. It runs with or without gameplay; the evidence ledger on the following pages lets a class complete every task from the printed evidence alone.

Learning target

I can use time-series records, a within-cycle profile, physical examination evidence, and habitat records to explain how removing a recurring environmental cue stopped a measured biological function, and I can write a schedule specification that would prevent the failure.

Measurable objectives

  • Separate the one condition that changed from the conditions two records show were held.
  • Read reported values, ranges, and day stamps without inventing unreported ones.
  • Read a within-cycle record and explain what a daily total cannot show.
  • Distinguish a compound being removed from a compound never being produced.
  • Distinguish established Earth chronobiology from records made for this grove.
  • Reject the filtration, light-damage, and transit-stress alternatives with evidence.
  • Write a dark-period specification with a monitored trial and a stop rule.

Success criteria

Data are exact

Reported values are copied without alteration. “40–80 ppb” stays a range. “0.0 ppb” is reported as a reading at the threshold, never as an absence of molecules.

Claims are bounded

Explanations say “entrained”, “recorded for this grove”, and name at least one thing the evidence does not establish.

Evidence converges

The written explanation draws on more than one source and does not rest on the timing of the change alone.

Design is testable

The specification names dark hours and cycle stability, and the trial has a measurement schedule and a stop rule.

Standards alignment and limitation

Direct assessment: MS-LS1-5. Students construct an evidence-based scientific explanation of how a local environmental condition — the presence or absence of a recurring dark interval — influences an organism.

Direct assessment: MS-ETS1-1. Task 8 defines criteria and constraints with enough precision to guide a solution, and it accounts for impacts on the people who live with the grove.

Supporting alignment: MS-ETS1-2, conditional. Claim this only if your class systematically compares restoring the six-hour schedule against adopting the five-hour recorded minimum using the same criteria and constraints. Do not claim direct assessment from the packet as written.

Assessment boundary

MS-LS1-5 names growth and genetic factors. This packet assesses the explanation practice and the local-conditions idea only. The records show growth was not affected, and no genetic factor is investigated, so do not report growth-rate or heredity evidence from this packet. The packet also does not assess the biochemistry of any clock mechanism.

Patterns

The within-cycle record, not the daily total, is what identifies the function as timed.

Stability and Change

Systems and System Models

Three organisms coordinate through one signal; the components stay intact while the coordination stops.

Where the two logs differ

The grove sensor change log gives Day −80 for the start of complete silence; the caretaker logs give Day −83. The difference is between two records of the same events, and the packet never asks students to reconcile it. Treat a student who notices the difference as having read carefully, and redirect the discussion to what both logs agree on: one condition changed, and nothing else did.

Claims to correct on sight

  • “All plants need darkness”, or “every plant needs a night to reset.” Responses to continuous light differ among species; the packet establishes a requirement for this grove only.
  • “Darkness is a nutrient.” It is a memorable phrase and not a claim the evidence supports. Darkness supplies no matter or energy; here it is a timing cue.
  • “0.0 ppb means there are no molecules”, or “the output is exactly zero.” It is a reading at the reporting threshold.
  • “The grove will sing again”, or “signalling will return in a day or two.” Recovery is what the proposed trial tests, not a result the packet reports.
  • “Continuous light always harms plants.” The cited Earth work reports both injury and tolerance depending on species and conditions.
  • “The organisms decided to stop speaking”, or “the grove chose dormancy.” The records describe a state, not an intention.
  • “All chemical signalling between organisms is circadian.” The records describe this grove’s signalling only.
  • “The clock thinks it is always midday.” That is narration. What the records show is a cycle with no recurring cue.
  • “Five dark hours is the requirement.” Five hours is the smallest interval that maintained signalling in this grove’s recorded trials, and the schedule that actually worked here used six.
  • “The caretakers damaged the grove.” Both logs report no structural decline; the organisms are healthy.
  • “The average healthy output is 60 ppb.” The record reports a 40–80 ppb range and no midpoint.
  • “The grove was singing.” Signalling is chemical in these records. Treat singing and harmonics as the crew’s way of speaking, not as an acoustic measurement.

Data-use cautions

  • Do not read a value for any hour block the within-cycle record marks as not reported, and do not draw a line between the reported blocks.
  • Do not replace the 40–80 ppb range with a single number.
  • Do not treat 0.0 ppb as an absolute absence; the sensor separately reports output falling below the measurable threshold.
  • Do not average or reconcile the two logs’ different silence-onset days.
  • Do not convert the recorded five-hour minimum into a specification number; the schedule with a two-year record used six dark hours.

Discussion guidance

  • Open with Task 1: name the one thing that changed, then ask why that is not yet an answer.
  • After Task 3, ask what a smooth line through the two reported blocks would have implied and why the packet refuses to draw one.
  • After Task 4, ask what a compound that is never produced would look like compared with one that is filtered out, and which evidence tells them apart.
  • Close with the procurement question: who should have written down what this grove needed before anyone changed the lights?

Accessibility and differentiation guidance

Each task occupies its own page with a data table placed beside every figure, one reading operation per prompt, and the written explanation on a dedicated page.

Why the order of events is not yet a cause: The signalling faded after the schedule changed, but a sequence in time is a correlation. It does not show how continuous light stops the signalling, and it does not rule out a second change that happened at the same time. A mechanism needs the within-cycle record, the examination evidence, and the habitat record as well.

Dark hours per cycle before and after Day −90: 6.0 h of dark before, 0.0 h after. Accept “six hours to none”.

Expected healthy output, copied exactly: 40–80 ppb, cycling. Do not accept a single value written in place of the range.

What the 0.0 ppb reading does and does not tell you: It tells you that the array can detect no signal at its reporting threshold, and the change log separately records output falling below the measurable threshold. It does not tell you that no molecules exist, and it does not by itself say whether production stopped or something removed the compounds.

What the within-cycle record shows — completed exemplar: Under 18 h on / 6 h off the output was not steady: the record reports a peak in hours 19–24, inside the dark block, and a minimum in hours 6–12, in the middle of the lit block, repeating for two years. A daily total would report only how much was released and would hide the fact that release was timed to a particular part of the cycle. Under 24 h on / 0 h off the record reports no cycling pattern at all.

Unreported blocks — completed exemplar: Hours 0–6, hours 12–18, and hours 18–19 are marked not separately reported. Any two of those three are acceptable. They may not be filled in because the records give no value for them; a number written there would be invented, and a line drawn between the reported blocks would claim a shape the record never measured.

CLAIM

Removing the grove’s recurring dark interval removed the timing cue that the signalling cycle recorded for this grove is entrained to, so measured output fell to the reporting threshold while the three organisms stayed healthy.

EVIDENCE

The schedule changed from 18.0 h on / 6.0 h off to 24.0 h on / 0.0 h off on Day −90, and both ship logs record no other change. Under the old schedule the record reports a signalling peak in hours 19–24, inside the dark block, and a minimum in hours 6–12, repeating for two years; under the new schedule it reports no cycling pattern and output of 0.0 ppb at the reporting threshold against an expected 40–80 ppb cycling range. The examination reports the release structures closed rather than damaged and the receiver intact, and this grove’s habitat records report a validated minimum of at least five dark hours with suppression over 7–10 days of continuous light.

REASONING

A daily total counts how much is released; it cannot show that release was timed. The within-cycle record shows the timing, and the schedule change removed the only recurring event that timing was tied to. Because the components are intact and every other condition held, the best-supported account is a cycle that lost its cue rather than an organism that was damaged or a compound that was filtered away. This remains the best-supported explanation rather than a proven mechanism: the records do not report values for every hour block, the two logs differ on when silence became complete, and the requirement for a dark interval was recorded for this grove rather than established for plants generally.

Criterion 1 — dark hours per cycle: Specify a recurring dark interval of at least 6.0 h per 24 h cycle. Accept answers that choose six rather than the recorded five-hour minimum and justify it: six is the interval with a two-year record of full signalling in this grove, while five is only the smallest interval that maintained signalling in trials, leaving no margin.

Criterion 2 — cycle stability: The dark interval must fall at the same point in every cycle and repeat without interruption, so the cue recurs on a fixed schedule rather than appearing at random.

Constraint — completed exemplar: Accept any constraint drawn from the records. Strong answers name the ship’s power supply, which already fluctuated once and must not be able to cancel the dark interval; the grove being a shared and culturally significant space, so caretakers still need access and working light; or the six-month reversibility window, which bounds how long the trial can take.

Monitored trial and stop rule: Restore the 18/6 schedule and measure signalling output on a fixed schedule, sampling within the dark block and within the lit block so a returning cycle can be distinguished from a rise in the total. Record whether output re-enters the 40–80 ppb range and whether it cycles. If output has not risen above the reporting threshold within the window the records give for reversibility, stop, restore the recorded conditions, and re-examine the diagnosis rather than lengthening the dark period further.

Uncertainty note: The investigation predicts that signalling resumes within a day or two. That is a narrated outcome, not a replicated trial, so the prediction is something the trial tests.
