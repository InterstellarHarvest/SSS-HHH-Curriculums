Student Mission
Mission question

Every vault reading sits inside the karreth specification and the bloom’s tissue is healthy, so why has compound production fallen to 6% of baseline — and what must a monitored trial include before anything is changed?

Case briefing

The Concord Botanical Vault at Lagrange Point 5 is the most heavily shielded facility in known space. A karreth bloom was transplanted there six months ago. The bloom makes medicinal compounds that treat a disease affecting several Concord species, and supplies are running out. The bloom grew normally for two months. Since month three its compound output has fallen every month, while the plant itself has stayed healthy.

Reasoning rule

Copy every reported value exactly. A reading of <0.01 mGy/day is a measurement at the monitor’s detection limit, not a measurement of zero. Keep about 8.4 mGy/day and about 12 mGy/day as they are reported, and say which one is modeled. Absorbed dose here is given in milligray (mGy); do not restate it in sievert. Do not write a value for any month or condition the records do not report.

A reading of zero

A rain gauge is marked only in whole millimetres. On two different nights it reads 0 mm. On the first night 0.2 mm of rain fell. On the second night 0.8 mm fell.

Rain fell on both nights, and the two amounts were not the same. The gauge reports the same thing for both because both are smaller than the smallest amount it can show.

This rain gauge is just an example. It is not a vault instrument.

SolarAgriculturalAgency
Teacher Guide

Purpose

Students diagnose why a healthy plant stopped making a valuable compound in a facility where every monitored condition reads correct, and then specify the trial that would have to happen before anyone acts on that diagnosis. The case turns on a condition the facility never wrote a biological specification for, and on the difference between a reading of zero and an amount too small to report.

The phenomenon

A karreth bloom transplanted into the Concord Botanical Vault produced normally for two months, then declined every month to 6% of baseline while remaining structurally healthy. The vault holds absorbed dose at plant tissue below its monitor’s 0.01 mGy/day detection limit. The homeworld site record reports about 8.4 mGy/day. Concord records describe a species-specific pathway in which a low-level radiation signal activates a repair cascade whose products include the medicinal compounds.

Measurable objectives

Separate conditions the facility monitored against a specification from the condition it never specified. Judge what a reading at a detection limit, a site record, and a modeled average can each support. Explain how a condition present from day one can produce a decline that appears later. Use adjustments that produced no change as evidence against three alternatives. Distinguish established Earth radiation science from records made for this case. Write an explanation that names its own limits. Specify a monitored trial with a control and a stop rule.

Success criteria

Readings are read correctly

<0.01 mGy/day is treated as a value below the monitor’s detection limit, never as zero, and never as exactly 0.01 mGy/day. The modeled Rhessi figure is labelled as modeled.

Units stay disciplined

Absorbed dose stays in milligray. No student converts or restates it in sievert, and no student turns a plant-tissue dose into a statement about human risk.

Null results count

The five adjustments that produced no change are used as evidence, not skipped as failures.

Claims are bounded

Explanations say the pathway is species-specific and name at least one thing the evidence does not establish — most often that the result transfers to other organisms.

Design is testable

The specification names a control that receives no added exposure, a measurement, and a written stop rule, and it leaves the exposure itself to the qualified team.

Standards alignment and limitation

Direct assessment: MS-ETS1-1. Task 7 defines the criteria and constraints of the trial with enough precision to guide a solution, and it accounts for impacts on people — the vault staff who would work near any exposure, and the patients across several species who depend on the compound. Students write two criteria, one constraint, a stop rule, and a recommendation with a reason. That is the task evidence for this standard.

Supporting alignment: MS-LS1-5, bounded. Task 6 is an evidence-based explanation of how an environmental condition influences an organism, which is the practice half of this performance expectation. Do not report it as direct assessment. The performance expectation names growth, and the records here show growth and tissue health were not affected; the affected output is a compound. The mechanism in this case is a regulated biosynthetic pathway, which falls outside this standard’s assessment boundary. Claim the explanation practice, not the performance expectation.

Supporting alignment: MS-ETS1-2, conditional. Claim this only if your class systematically compares repairing this vault against amending Protocol v3.2 using one shared set of criteria. The packet as written asks for a recommendation with a reason, which is not a systematic comparison.

Science and engineering practices

Analyzing and Interpreting Data — Tasks 2 and 3. Engaging in Argument from Evidence — Tasks 3, 4 and 5. Developing and Using Models — the Task 5 mechanism model. Constructing Explanations and Designing Solutions — Tasks 6 and 7. Asking Questions and Defining Problems — Tasks 1 and 7.

Crosscutting concepts

Scale, Proportion, and Quantity

Task 2 is built on it: a detection limit, a site record and a modeled average are three different kinds of number, and the gap between <0.01 and about 8.4 mGy/day is the case.

Cause and Effect

Tasks 1 and 3 keep a correlation and a set of null results separate from a demonstrated mechanism. Two dose points are not a response curve.

Stability and Change

Task 3 asks how an unchanging condition produces a changing output — the hardest idea in the case.

Systems and System Models

Task 5 models a signal, a pathway, and a product, with the plant’s other systems left intact.

The science boundary

This is the case in the campaign most likely to be over-generalized, and the over-generalization is not harmless. Hold three lines.

Line 1 — the pathway is species-specific

Concord records classify the karreth bloom under a rare category in which a low-level radiation signal activates one species’ metabolic pathway. The records state plainly that the mechanisms are species-specific and should not be generalized. Nothing in this case supports a claim that ionizing radiation benefits plants in general, Earth organisms, or people. Radiation protection remains the correct default everywhere outside this one documented pathway.

Line 2 — the Earth comparison is narrower than it looks

Melanin is well established as radioprotective in fungi. A 2007 laboratory study reported that ionizing radiation changed melanin’s electronic properties and enhanced growth or metabolic activity in several melanized fungi under specific conditions, and the authors described energy capture as an intriguing possibility. That is where the established finding stops. It is not a demonstration of radiation-powered photosynthesis, and it does not show that any fungus requires radiation. Use it as a reason the karreth scenario is not absurd, never as proof that it is real.

Line 3 — the numbers are bounded in three different ways

<0.01 mGy/day bounds a value from above. About 8.4 mGy/day is a record from one surveyed site, not an optimum. About 12 mGy/day is modeled, for a different species. Two dose conditions and one outcome do not make a response curve, which is exactly why Task 7 asks for a staged trial that finds a minimum rather than a plan that copies the homeworld number.

Provenance language

The packet says vault records, site record, species record, modeled average, recorded outcome. It does not repeatedly remind students that the scenario is invented; that framing teaches nothing and undercuts the reasoning. Where a boundary matters, the packet names the boundary instead — whose record it is, and what that kind of record can support.

Claims to correct on sight

Each of these will appear. The quotation is the student version; the sentence after it is the correction.

  • “Radiation is a nutrient for this plant.” It is a memorable phrase and it is not what the record describes. A nutrient is consumed and built into the organism. What the record describes is a signal that switches a pathway on. Ask the student what the plant would be made of if the phrase were true.
  • “So a little radiation is good for you.” The case gives no support for this. The Concord record explicitly says its category is not a general claim that low-dose radiation benefits Earth organisms, and nothing in the packet measures a human response. This is the single most important correction on the page.
  • “The vault has zero radiation.” The vault monitor reports below 0.01 mGy/day, which is a statement about the instrument as much as about the vault. Return the student to Figure A and the two nights that both read 0 mm.
  • “Fungi at Chernobyl eat radiation like plants eat sunlight.” The laboratory work reported changed melanin properties and enhanced growth under particular conditions, and proposed energy capture as a possibility. Radiation-powered photosynthesis was not established, and no fungus was shown to require radiation.
  • “More radiation would make more medicine.” The case has two dose conditions and gives no response curve. More exposure could as easily damage the bloom. This is precisely the assumption Task 7 is designed to prevent.
  • “So the answer is 8.4 mGy/day.” That is the value recorded at the surveyed homeworld site. Nothing shows it is the smallest exposure that works, or the best one. A staged trial finds the minimum; a worksheet does not.
  • “The shielding was a mistake and the Concord was wrong to build it.” The vault protected the bloom from a real hazard using the best standard available. The failure was a missing step — no species-specific biological review — not the decision to shield. Students who reach for blame usually miss the actual design lesson.
Marking note

Accept any rejection of an alternative that cites a specific record. Do not require the word “control” in Task 7 before students have met it; require the idea — a chamber treated the same way except for the exposure.

Task notes 1–4

Task 1 · Sort what was specified from what was not

Five rows are M and one is N. The N row is the whole case. Students who mark radiation as M usually mean “it was measured” — press them: measured against what specification? Protocol v3.2 states none for this species. The written answer should say that a report of “all optimal” only covers what somebody chose to specify.

Task 2 · What a reading can and cannot tell you

Run the rain-gauge example aloud before students read the table. The three prompts are deliberately different: the first is about a bound, the second about units, the third about how a number was produced. For the units prompt, accept any answer saying the two quantities measure different things and that converting needs weighting factors the case never gives.

Task 3 · The timeline, and null results as evidence

The hardest prompt in the packet is the middle one. The shielding did not change; what changed was the bloom’s accumulated state. Accept any answer that separates the constant condition from the accumulating consequence. Students who answer “the shielding got stronger” have misread the record — send them back to Table 1. The third prompt turns five failed adjustments into evidence rather than a list of defeats.

Task 4 · Convergence, and the limit column

The limit column is the point of the task. Any single source is weak: the admin has a motive and a hypothesis, the sensor array shows an absence, the specimen shows a quiescent pathway without a cause, the transplant record describes a different place, and the database describes a category rather than this plant. Together they converge.

Task notes 5–7

Task 5 · Diagnosis, rejection, and mechanism

Option 1 is B. The three rejections should each cite a specific record — the reformulations and matched supplements, the in-specification climate readings that were also varied, and the two months at 100% followed by a steady decline. The word bank then forces the causal order: signal falls, pathway stays off, product is not made.

Task 5 · A common inversion

Watch for students who reverse Stages 3 and 4. That usually means they think the compound is the repair rather than a product made downstream of it. Ask what the pathway would be repairing.

Task 6 · CER on its own page

Require the named limit. The most common weak CER cites only the two dose values and skips the eliminations, which makes the argument a correlation. A strong CER uses at least three sources and says what it cannot conclude.

Task 7 · Specification, not a recipe

Students specify what the trial must include and what would stop it. They do not choose a source, a device, or a setting, and the packet says so on the page. If a student writes an operating plan, redirect to the criterion: what would you measure, and what result would make you stop? Both recommendations are acceptable when the reason is grounded; the policy answer should still keep the trial requirement.

Running the packet

Two class periods. Tasks 1–3 sit naturally in the first, Tasks 4–7 in the second. X so the explanation is written in one sitting rather than assembled in margins. Students who have played the case will recognise the five sources; students who have not can work entirely from the printed records.

Accessible edition

The Accessible Mission carries the same seven tasks, the same identifiers, the same evidence and the same diagnosis, with one task per page. It adds a vocabulary list before Task 1, sentence frames, word banks, and shorter directions, and it splits multi-part prompts so only one comparison is open at a time. It does not name the diagnosis before Task 6, and its Task 6 keeps the full Claim–Evidence–Reasoning structure on a single page. Students may move between editions task by task; the identifiers match.

A note on framing

This case ends with a policy recommendation, and it is worth naming why. A universal safety standard was written by people who were not wrong about the hazard and who never asked whether any organism needed the thing they were removing. The vault administrator, who had the relevant expertise, was assigned after construction. That is an ordinary engineering failure — a missing review step — and it is a better lesson than a story about a villain. Keep the discussion on the process that was missing.

Production first falls below baseline in month 3, to 68%. Months 1 and 2 are both at 100%, so the bloom was capable of normal production inside this vault. That rules out damage during the move and rules out an immediate incompatibility with the vault.

CLAIM

The vault’s shielding reduced absorbed dose at plant tissue below the level this species’ compound pathway responds to, and that is the best-supported explanation for the fall in production.

EVIDENCE

The vault holds absorbed dose at plant tissue below 0.01 mGy/day, at the detection limit, while the homeworld site record reports about 8.4 mGy/day. Every other specified condition reads inside the karreth range, and five separate adjustments produced no change. The examination record shows healthy tissue with the pathway present but quiescent. Concord records describe a species-specific pathway in which a low-level radiation signal activates the repair cascade that makes the compounds. Production fell from 100% to 6% of baseline across months 3 to 6.

REASONING

The vault was built to remove a hazard and succeeded, but no one wrote a species-specific requirement for the thing being removed. Because the plant is healthy and the pathway is intact but switched off, the failure is a missing signal rather than damage or a shortage. The alternatives are weakened by adjustments that changed nothing. This evidence does not establish that ionizing radiation benefits other organisms, does not give a response curve from two dose conditions, and does not show that about 8.4 mGy/day is the right exposure to use — which is why the next step is a controlled trial rather than a change.

  • Criterion 1 — measure absorbed dose actually reaching plant tissue with remote dosimetry, and measure compound production on a fixed schedule. Dose at the tissue is the quantity in the diagnosis, and production is the outcome it is supposed to explain.
  • Criterion 2 — keep a chamber that receives no added exposure. Without it, any recovery could follow some other change, or could be the bloom recovering on its own.
  • Constraint — accept any of: the work needs licensed radiation-protection and radiological-engineering approval; shielding and interlocks must stay in place; staff exposure limits apply; supplies of the compound are running out, so the trial is under time pressure; only a small number of specimens exist.
  • Smallest effective exposure — about 8.4 mGy/day is what was recorded at one surveyed site, not a value shown to be necessary or best. Two dose conditions give no response curve, and more exposure could damage the bloom, so a staged trial should find the lowest exposure that restores production.
  • Recommendation — both answers are acceptable when reasoned. Repairing the vault alone fixes this bloom and leaves the standard unchanged. Recommending a species-specific validation step in Protocol v3.2 addresses the missing review that produced the failure, while keeping radiation protection as the default. A strong answer keeps the trial requirement either way.
