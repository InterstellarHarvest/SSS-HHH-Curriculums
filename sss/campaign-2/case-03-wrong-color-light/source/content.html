Student Mission
Mission question

The dome lamps are bright and the water readings are nominal, so why is the zhal-kelp declining — and what would a better lighting specification have to include?

Case briefing

A new Oolian mariculture dome at Trench Shelf IV replaced its Oolian OMS-4 fixtures with Earth-manufactured GRO-9 grow lights. The Week 0 dome record lists that fixture change as the only change: the water and salinity line, the kelp species, and the nutrient supply and feed line were all kept as they were in the old dome. Zhal-kelp that was healthy in the old dome began yellowing within a week and has declined for six weeks. Oolian representatives say the human equipment is defective. The supplier says the equipment meets its written specification.

Reasoning rule

Total PAR counts every photon in the range plants use. It does not show how that light is spread across wavelengths, so total PAR alone does not establish an effective spectrum: compare the lamp-output evidence and the kelp-response evidence separately. The zhal-kelp values in this packet were measured at this dome; they are not established Earth biology. Copy every “less than” value exactly as written, and do not read values between the reported categories.

Teacher Guide

Lesson overview

Students investigate why zhal-kelp is declining in a new Oolian mariculture dome after Earth-made GRO-9 grow lights replaced the Oolian OMS-4 fixtures. Water chemistry, species, and feed line were controlled; only the light source changed. The investigation separates how much light from which wavelengths, and ends with a written lighting specification and a monitored test.

The packet is built for grades 6–8 and adapts upward for grades 9–12 and adult learners. It runs with or without gameplay; the essential evidence ledger on the following pages lets a class complete every task from the printed evidence alone.

Learning target

I can use spectral data, plant evidence, controls, and habitat records to explain why a bright light source can still be a poor match for a particular organism, and I can write a specification that would prevent the failure.

Measurable objectives

  • Distinguish total light quantity (PAR) from spectral composition.
  • Read discrete reported percentages and wavelength ranges without inventing unreported values.
  • Explain that photosynthetic systems can respond differently across wavelengths.
  • Distinguish established Earth science from case-specific measurements.
  • Combine evidence from all five sources and state what each cannot establish alone.
  • Reject the defective-equipment, low-intensity, and water-chemistry alternatives with evidence.
  • Write a species-specific lighting specification with a monitored trial and a stop rule.

Success criteria

Data are exact

Reported values are copied without alteration. “Less than 5%” stays “less than 5%”. “Below detection” is not written as zero.

Claims are bounded

Explanations say “poor match”, “strongest measured range”, and name at least one thing the evidence does not establish.

Evidence converges

The written explanation draws on more than one source and does not rest on a single percentage.

Design is testable

The specification names spectrum and intensity, and the trial has a measurement schedule and a stop rule.

Standards alignment and limitation

Direct assessment: MS-LS1-6. Task 7 requires a written, evidence-based explanation of why captured energy rather than delivered brightness limits zhal-kelp growth, and Task 6 completes the condition-mechanism-effect model that explanation rests on. Tasks 2, 3, and 5 supply the light data and plant response it must cite.

Supporting alignment: MS-ETS1-1. Task 8 defines a spectral criterion, an intensity criterion, and one constraint with enough precision to guide a solution. It is supporting rather than direct because the Student task sets no impacts-on-people expectation.

Supporting alignment: MS-ETS1-2, conditional. Task 8 is the only place this could be assessed, and only if your class systematically compares retuning against replacement using the same criteria and constraints. Do not claim direct assessment of MS-ETS1-2 from the packet as written.

Assessment boundary

Do not claim biochemical-detail mastery beyond the MS-LS1-6 assessment boundary. The packet does not assess pigment chemistry, energy-transfer mechanisms, or quantitative photon accounting. MS-PS4-2 is not claimed. Students read reported wavelength bands and lamp-output figures, but no task develops or uses a model of light reflected, absorbed, or transmitted through a material, so wavelength vocabulary and figure reading alone do not assess that performance expectation.

Crosscutting concepts

Cause and Effect

Correlation in time is separated from mechanism throughout Tasks 1, 4, and 6.

Systems and System Models

Structure and Function

Energy and Matter

Captured energy, not delivered photons, drives growth.

Claims to correct on sight

  • “Kelp cannot use red light.” The case does not support an absolute claim; the installed mix is inefficient for this measured response.
  • “Red light is unused”, or “red light is the same as darkness.” Response outside 460–540 nm is not specified and is not zero.
  • “All brown algae share one action spectrum.” Responses vary; no single universal peak describes all photosynthetic systems.
  • “460–540 nm is the only usable range.” It is the strongest measured range for one species at one site.
  • “The <5% figure proves the diagnosis.” One reported percentage is not a diagnosis.
  • “Effective PAR is 14 µmol/m²/s.” The investigation reports approximately that value and does not report how it was obtained. Treat it as an approximate figure, not an exact organism-level result, and never as a calculation students perform or check.
  • “Below detection means zero.” It means below the limit the instrument can report.
  • “The kelp will recover within days.” That is a narrated prediction, not a replicated trial.
  • “Pigment evolution takes millions of years.” The packet makes no universal claim about evolutionary timetables.

Data-use cautions

  • Do not infer spectral overlap geometrically from the reported categories. The categories are coarse and their edges differ between fixtures; the <5% figure in the strongest-response band is separately reported by the case, not derived from bar widths.
  • Do not sum or interpolate unreported values.
  • Keep <5% and <1% as inequalities.
  • Treat “below detection” as below the reporting limit, not zero.
  • Keep the approximate 14 µmol/m²/s effective-PAR figure out of student work entirely; it is noted here only so you recognise it if a student meets it in the investigation.

The Wrong Color of Light

Answer Key · Continued
SolarAgriculturalAgency

CLAIM

The installed red-heavy GRO-9 lamps are a poor spectral match for the response measured for zhal-kelp at this site, so the kelp captures far less energy than the total brightness suggests.

EVIDENCE

GRO-9 reports 62% of output at 620–680 nm and <5% in the blue-green category, while the strongest measured response for zhal-kelp at this site is 460–540 nm and the retired OMS-4 put 78% of output there. Temperature, salinity, pH, oxygen, nutrients, species, and feed line were unchanged, and the old dome still grows at 100%. The specimen’s photosynthetic structures are intact.

REASONING

Total PAR counts photons across the whole range; it does not report whether an organism can use them. Because most of the installed output sits where this species is measured to respond weakly, captured energy falls even though brightness is adequate, and less energy is available for growth and pigment replacement. This is the best-supported explanation rather than a proven mechanism: response outside 460–540 nm is not specified and is not zero, and the 460–540 nm band was measured at this site rather than established as Earth biology.

Criterion 1 — spectrum: The fixture must deliver a majority of its output within the 460–540 nm range measured for zhal-kelp at this site, verified against the fixture’s own reported output categories.

Criterion 2 — total intensity: Total PAR at the canopy must stay at or above the 280 µmol/m²/s the case already reports as adequate, so the spectral change does not create a brightness problem.

Constraint — completed exemplar: Accept solutions that work with the existing dome hardware and power budget; the installed fixtures support custom spectra, so retuning and replacement should both be considered against the same criteria.

Monitored trial and stop rule: Run the change on part of the dome first and keep the remaining tanks on the current fixtures as a comparison. Measure delivered spectrum, total PAR, blade colour, and growth rate on a fixed schedule. If yellowing continues or growth does not improve over the agreed window, stop, restore the comparison condition, and re-examine the diagnosis rather than increasing brightness.

Uncertainty note: The case predicts recovery within days. That is a narrated outcome, not a replicated experiment, so the prediction must be treated as something the trial tests.
