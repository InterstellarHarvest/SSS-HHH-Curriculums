The documented record · what really happened

G · Vavilov, the collection and the siege SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

Where it comes from. The Crop Trust, the international organisation that supports the world's genebanks.

  • Nikolai Vavilov was born in Moscow in 1887. Between 1916 and 1933 he made 115 collecting expeditions to 64 countries on five continents, and in 1926 he published the theory that every crop has a centre of origin.
  • He amassed more than 250,000 seed samples — the world's largest repository of crop diversity.
  • He was arrested in 1940, during a collecting expedition in western Ukraine, and accused of being a traitor and a spy. A death sentence was commuted to twenty years' imprisonment.
  • He died in incarceration in Saratov on 26 January 1943. The cause was starvation.
  • During the 900-day siege of Leningrad the Institute's staff refused to eat the seeds even as they starved to death.
  • Genebanks, collecting expeditions and the use of plant genetic resources in breeding are now standard parts of the food system.

It gives no month for the arrest, no count of the staff who died and no names. It establishes the dates; it establishes nothing about the game.

H · The Institute's own wartime record SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

Where it comes from. I. G. Loskutov, writing in 2021 in the Institute's own journal from its archives: the wartime orders, the staff personal files and the papers of the keepers.

  • The failed evacuation. The invasion began on 22 June 1941. Evacuation of staff and collection was scheduled for 25 August; on 27 August the last railway was cut, and on 8 September 1941 the siege ring closed. The staff came back into the city, and the freight car loaded with the seed collection stayed on a siding.
  • Divided and sealed. The returned collection was split into two lots kept in different parts of the building, so that one bomb could not destroy both; the boxes were tied into packs, moved into sealed rooms and inventoried. Keys were held by a named superintendent. Seals were checked daily, boxes weekly. The potatoes, dug unripe at Pavlovsk under fire, went into a sealed basement that was never allowed to freeze.
  • The people. Famine began in November 1941; the winter ration fell to 125 grams of bread mixed with bran. Staff died of hunger and exhaustion at their posts — the head of the rice section in his office in January 1942, beside several thousand packages of rice seed; the keeper of the oats in the room where the oats were stored. They did not use the rice, pea, maize and wheat accessions to relieve their hunger. The article counts more than twenty experts and scientists whose lives the preservation cost, and more than thirty researchers lost in the first autumn alone to bombing, starvation and the front.
  • Moved, reproduced, checked. Part of the potato collection was flown to Krasnoufimsk, in the Urals, in November 1941. Staff were evacuated over Lake Ladoga from February 1942 carrying seed, and about 40,000 packages and a duplicate potato set were flown out in March. More than 100,000 accessions were kept at Krasnoufimsk through the siege. In Leningrad the potatoes and about 200 cereal varieties were planted in suburban fields under fire in 1942, and again in 1943, because seed that sits damp and cold loses its power to germinate. The first staff came back from Krasnoufimsk in February 1944. In 1946 the whole collection was checked and an emergency regeneration programme for the weakest accessions was carried through, with every station involved.
  • What was lost. One potato cultivar from the basement; germination in some subtropical and highland accessions; several Chilean varieties and wild species, later revived from other stations and from the seed store; and, across the whole war, about 40,000 accessions, including collections captured at Pushkin and fruit collections lost at Pavlovsk.

It establishes what happened to the real collection. It establishes nothing about the game's keeper, scan, ledger or report, and its count of the dead is one archival account's count; other published accounts count differently.

I · The Institute today SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

The N. I. Vavilov All-Russian Institute of Plant Genetic Resources (VIR), a Federal Research Center, still holds the collection today under Vavilov's name.

Present identity only. The page as consulted carried no siege narrative, and this packet borrows none from it.

Launch sheet

This case runs over three ordinary class periods. Students decide whether a seed collection survived the Siege of Leningrad as the same continuing collection, and say what each source can and cannot prove about it. The siege, the collection, Vavilov's arrest and death, the staff who starved beside the seed, and the postwar regeneration are historical and documented. The keeper, the Archive's scan and ledger, the recovered recording and the Consumption Report were written for the game.

Before class

Print the eight-page Student Mission or the ten-page Accessible Mission. Either packet carries the whole reconstructed dossier, all three documented source cards and both figures, so either is completely teachable with no device in the room. Read the source-status notice on learner page 1 yourself first, and plan to read it aloud. The content is emotionally heavy: people died of hunger at their desks. Say so plainly and once.

The one thing to say out loud on day one

Vavilov built the collection and was not there to keep it. He was arrested in 1940 and died in prison in Saratov in January 1943, while his colleagues in Leningrad kept the collection through the siege. A class that finishes this unit believing Vavilov starved in the vault has learned the wrong story, however well it answers everything else.

Essential evidence

Two documented sources carry this case, and they carry different halves of it. Source G, the Crop Trust, supplies the dates that fix Vavilov's strand: arrested in 1940, dead in Saratov on 26 January 1943. Source H, Loskutov's archival history in the Institute's own journal, supplies everything about the collection: the failed evacuation, the division and sealing, the partial evacuation to Krasnoufimsk, the re-sowing under fire, the deaths, the losses and the 1946 check. 3 · Separate Vavilov's Timeline from the Siege is built on G; 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis and 7 · Make a Collection Continuity Judgment are built on H.

Likely sticking point

Two, and they pull in opposite directions. The game's scan says the seed never left the room, and students carry that over as history. The documented record says the collection was divided, partly flown out, replanted and partly lost — and students then conclude it cannot be the same collection. 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis exists to close both: continuity does not require immobility or perfect survival.

What to collect

Collect the whole packet at the end of period 3. 7 · Make a Collection Continuity Judgment is the graded culminating product; it requires a judgment about the real collection, the strongest documented links, one explicit limit on testimony or reconstructed evidence, and why crop diversity mattered beyond the siege. 8 · Transfer the Method is the transfer check, answered about no particular collection.

Teacher framing line

“A city was starving, and inside it was a building full of seed that could have been eaten. The people who kept it did not eat it, and some of them died a room away from it. This week you are going to do two things with that. First, work out from the records what actually happened to the seed — it moved, it was replanted, some of it was lost — and decide whether it is still the same collection. Second, say which of the things you know come from a documented record, and which come from a story a game told you.”

Classroom and technical fallback

If devices fail mid-period, switch to the no-game route and change nothing else. If you lose a period, protect 3 · Separate Vavilov's Timeline from the Siege, 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis and 7 · Make a Collection Continuity Judgment; 1 · Build the Case Vocabulary can be finished as homework and 5 · Compare What the Sources Can Establish can be worked in pairs. Dropping 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis is the one cut that breaks the lesson, because it is the only place continuity is built rather than named.

Complete teaching procedure

Three periods of about fifty minutes. Every numbered Student task appears below in the order the packet prints it. If your periods are shorter, move the reading in period 1 to homework rather than cutting a task.

  1. Period 1 · Launch — 6 min. Read the source-status notice on learner page 1 aloud. Say the rule in your own words: six of the sources were written for a game, three are real published records, and neither kind proves anything about the other. Post the guiding question.
  2. Period 1 · 1 · Build the Case Vocabulary — 8 min. Exact-match bank, seven terms, seven statements, no decoys. Circulate for one thing only: whether accession has landed as the numbered unit a collection is counted in, because the ledger, the losses and the regeneration are all counted in it.
  3. Period 1 · Read Sources A to F — 14 min. Learner pages 2 and 3. Read Source B aloud; let students read the rest. Say once, before they start, that all six were written for the game, and that the keeper is not a real person.
  4. Period 1 · 2 · Set a Continuity Test — 8 min. Provisional, and deliberately taken before the documented sources arrive. There is no correct answer and none is keyed. Read the two slots as a diagnostic: a class that names only the seeds themselves has not yet thought about records, and that is the useful gap the next page fills.
  5. Period 1 · Read Sources G, H and I — 12 min. Learner page 4. Read the last bullet of Source H aloud — what was lost. It is the sentence the rest of the case turns on, because continuity has to survive it.
  6. Period 2 · 3 · Separate Vavilov's Timeline from the Siege — 16 min. Part B is the one to protect. A learner who writes that he was in prison throughout, and that a record placing him in the besieged building is therefore impossible, has done the task; a learner who writes only that he died in 1943 has not.
  7. Period 2 · 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis — 24 min. The load-bearing task. Do box 2 of Part A together as a class, out loud, before releasing the rest — the answer has to name a record or a custodian, not a place, or the misconception survives the lesson.
  8. Period 3 · 5 · Compare What the Sources Can Establish — 16 min. Sixteen fields. Work row B together, then release. Watch for a class treating the recovered record and the documented record as equally strong because their dates agree; the agreement runs one way.
  9. Period 3 · 6 · Test the Consumption Report — 12 min. Say once, before they start, that the four tests use Sources B to E only and that the clean finish is not one of them. The commonest failure is a verdict reached from the typing.
  10. Period 3 · 7 · Make a Collection Continuity Judgment — 16 min. The graded culminating product. Enforce one rule while circulating: Part A is a judgment about the real collection, and it must survive Source H's losses rather than ignore them.
  11. Period 3 · 8 · Transfer the Method — 6 min. Transfer. Say once, before they start, that the answer must not be about Leningrad.
  12. Period 3 · Close and collect — 4 min. Ask for one sentence naming something the class could not settle. Collect the whole packet.

Facilitation prompts

When a student says the collection cannot be the same because it moved

Ask: “When the class library is moved to a new room over the summer, is it a new library?” Then ask what would make it a new one — and listen for the records. The continuity chain labels every link with the record or custodian that carried identity across it.

When a class stalls on 5 · Compare What the Sources Can Establish

Ask what each source would still be true of if the game had never been written. One of the four survives that question unchanged, one survives it only because a document happens to agree with it, and two do not survive it at all. That is the column they are being asked to fill.

Transitions and collection points

The three natural stopping points are the end of the reading in period 1, the end of 4 · Trace the Collection Through Crisis in period 2, and collection at the end of period 3. Nothing needs collecting before the end: 2 · Set a Continuity Test is provisional and is read rather than marked, and every later task depends on the packet staying with the learner.

G · Vavilov, the collection and the siege SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

Where it comes from. The Crop Trust, the international organisation that supports the world's genebanks.

  • Nikolai Vavilov was born in Moscow in 1887. Between 1916 and 1933 he made 115 collecting expeditions to 64 countries on five continents. In 1926 he published the theory that every crop has a centre of origin.
  • He amassed more than 250,000 seed samples — the world's largest repository of crop diversity.
  • He was arrested in 1940, during a collecting expedition in western Ukraine. He was accused of being a traitor and a spy. A death sentence was commuted to twenty years in prison.
  • He died in incarceration in Saratov on 26 January 1943. The cause was starvation.
  • During the 900-day siege of Leningrad the Institute's staff refused to eat the seeds even as they starved to death.
  • Genebanks and the use of plant genetic resources in breeding are now standard parts of the food system.

It gives no month for the arrest, no count of the staff who died and no names. It establishes the dates. It establishes nothing about the game.

The documented record · part 2 · the collection

H · The Institute's own wartime record SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

Where it comes from. I. G. Loskutov, writing in 2021 in the Institute's own journal, from its archives: the wartime orders, the staff files and the keepers' papers.

The failed evacuation.

  • The invasion began on 22 June 1941. Evacuation was scheduled for 25 August. On 27 August the last railway was cut.
  • On 8 September 1941 the siege ring closed. The staff came back into the city. The freight car loaded with the seed collection stayed on a siding.

Divided and sealed.

  • The returned collection was split into two lots, kept in different parts of the building, so one bomb could not destroy both. Boxes were tied into packs, moved into sealed rooms, and inventoried.
  • Keys were held by a named superintendent. Seals were checked daily, boxes weekly. The potatoes, dug unripe at Pavlovsk under fire, went into a sealed basement that was never allowed to freeze.

The people.

  • Famine began in November 1941. The winter ration fell to 125 grams of bread mixed with bran.
  • Staff died of hunger and exhaustion at their posts: the head of the rice section in his office in January 1942, beside several thousand packages of rice seed; the keeper of the oats in the room where the oats were stored.
  • They did not use the rice, pea, maize and wheat accessions to relieve their hunger.
  • The article counts more than twenty experts and scientists whose lives the preservation cost, and more than thirty researchers lost in the first autumn alone to bombing, starvation and the front.

Moved, reproduced, checked.

  • Part of the potato collection was flown to Krasnoufimsk, in the Urals, in November 1941. Staff were evacuated over Lake Ladoga from February 1942 carrying seed. About 40,000 packages and a duplicate potato set were flown out in March. More than 100,000 accessions were kept at Krasnoufimsk.
  • In Leningrad the potatoes and about 200 cereal varieties were planted in suburban fields under fire in 1942, and again in 1943, because seed that sits damp and cold loses its power to germinate.
  • The first staff came back from Krasnoufimsk in February 1944. In 1946 the whole collection was checked and an emergency regeneration programme for the weakest accessions was carried through.

What was lost. One potato cultivar from the basement; germination in some subtropical and highland accessions; several Chilean varieties and wild species, later revived from other stations; and, across the whole war, about 40,000 accessions, including collections captured at Pushkin and fruit collections lost at Pavlovsk.

It establishes what happened to the real collection. It establishes nothing about the game's keeper, scan, ledger or report. Its count of the dead is one archival account's count; other published accounts count differently.
