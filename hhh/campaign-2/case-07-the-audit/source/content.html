The method behind the case

The real discipline behind this case SOURCE STATUS · DOCUMENTED

Judging whether a document is what it claims to be is a real field with a name: diplomatics. The Society of American Archivists' dictionary defines it, and identifies Jean Mabillon's De Re Diplomatica (1681) in that entry. It describes the work as weighing a document's context with its internal and external characteristics — not as one surface test.

The U.S. National Archives describes a trustworthy record through four separate properties — reliability, authenticity, integrity, usability. Kept apart on purpose: a record can be authentic and still incomplete, or complete and still unreliable.

  • Authenticity is judged from several kinds of evidence at once — materials, handwriting, provenance, custody and corroboration.
  • Unexplained neatness may prompt closer examination. But no single surface feature proves authenticity or forgery. A clean record can be authentic, and a forged record can imitate corrections.

This card is real. The TAA cards are not. A real principle tells you how to reason about the TAA case; it is never evidence that the case happened.

Teacher Guide

Launch sheet

This case runs over three ordinary class periods. It is the first case of Campaign 2, and the job has changed: in Campaign 1 students explained what happened, and here they judge which record to believe. Everything about the Temporal Agricultural Archive is invented. The method students use on it is not.

Before class

Print the eight-page Student Mission or the ten-page Accessible Mission. Either packet carries the whole dossier, the real-world reference card and all three figures, so either is completely teachable with no device in the room. Read the source-status notice on learner page 1 yourself first, and plan to read it aloud.

The correct record, and why

Copy B is the better-supported record, and the reason is never one thing. Its custody trail records a recall and a re-filing that Copy A's does not; the reason recorded at the time matches a correction visible on its page; and the audit log — whose own custody is verified — establishes that the buried forgeries are expert, so a finished-looking copy proves nothing. Copy A's history is corroborated by nothing at all.

The two routes

Either route assesses the same eight tasks against the same rubric
RouteWhat students doWhat you must still supply

In the Briefing Chamber they take the audit briefing, hear the second archivist's account of the pattern, and read the audit log; at the Thread Console they examine the two copies and compare their custody trails, then validate the record.Check first that your class can reach it. Campaign 2 has no level selector and no shortcut; a class arrives at this level only by finishing the first campaign in the game. If yours has not, use the other route — it is not a reduced version.

Read the source-status notice aloud: what is invented, what is real, and the rule that neither proves anything about the other.

Essential evidence

One pairing carries this case, and it is a pairing rather than a single record. Copy B's page carries a corrected grain count. Copy B's custody trail carries a recall whose recorded purpose was to correct a grain count. Two records, made at different times by different processes, saying the same thing. Neither is worth much alone — a correction on a page is a surface feature, and an extra custody entry with no reason attached shows only that a record moved. Put them together and you have corroboration. 4 · Trace Each Copy's Provenance and 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record are built so that a learner has to name that pair and say why one half of it will not do.

Likely sticking point

Students arrive at the right answer by the wrong route, in one move: “the messy one is real because forgers are neat.” It gets Copy B and it would get a forgery too, the moment a forger thought of adding a crossing-out. 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof exists to close it before 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record opens, and the Answer Key refuses the single-factor route at every level.

What to collect

Collect the whole packet at the end of period 3. 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record is the graded culminating product; it requires two evidence links from different sources, and 8 · Test What Would Change Your Confidence is the transfer check, answered about a record no source in this packet mentions.

Teacher framing line

“Here are two documents. They both claim the same author. One of them is a forgery good enough to fool an archive for years. You are not going to catch it by looking at it harder — the person who made it was better at looking than you are. You are going to catch it by asking what else agrees with it, and by the end of the week you'll be able to say which one the evidence supports, and why the answer has nothing to do with which page looks better.”

Classroom and technical fallback

If devices fail mid-period, switch to the no-game route and change nothing else. If you lose a period, protect 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof, 4 · Trace Each Copy's Provenance and 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record; 1 · Build the Case Vocabulary can be finished as homework and 5 · Weigh the Four Evidence Sources can be worked in pairs. Dropping 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof is the one cut that breaks the lesson — without it a class reaches the right record by exactly the reasoning the case exists to refuse. If you must cut it, that is a modified assessment route and rubric criterion 2 can no longer be scored honestly.

Lesson overview

Three class periods. Students learn six terms, read four invented archive records and one real-world archival reference under printed status lines, sort six observations into what raises a question, what counts once corroborated and what cannot decide anything at all, trace and compare two recorded custody trails without inventing the history the records do not contain, decide what each of four sources establishes and what it cannot establish alone, set two competing copies of one report against each other on four matched dimensions, write a record validation memorandum that names the better-supported copy with two evidence links from different sources and one clue that looks like proof and is not, and then apply the same method to a record none of the sources mention.

Guiding historical question

Which report is better supported as authentic, and how does the chain of evidence justify that judgment?

Measurable objectives

  • Apply six archival terms to the things and relationships they name.
  • Classify an observation about a record by what it can carry: a question, evidence once corroborated, or nothing either way.
  • Trace two recorded custody chains, identify the meaningful differences, and state what neither chain records.
  • State for each of four sources what it establishes, what it cannot establish alone, and its evidentiary role.
  • Judge which of two competing records is better supported, using at least two evidence links from different sources, and state a limitation.
  • Name evidence that would raise or lower confidence in the authenticity of an unfamiliar record, and say why.

Complete teaching procedure

Three periods of about fifty minutes. Every numbered Student task appears below in the order the packet prints it. If your periods are shorter, move the reading in period 1 to homework rather than cutting a task.

  1. Period 1 · Launch — 6 min. Read the source-status notice on learner page 1 aloud. Say the rule in your own words: the Archive is invented, the archival principle is real, and neither proves anything about the other. Post the guiding question.
  2. Period 1 · 1 · Build the Case Vocabulary — 8 min. Exact-match bank, six terms, six statements, no decoys. Circulate for one thing only: whether provenance and chain of custody have landed as different ideas. Task 4 needs the difference.
  3. Period 1 · Read Sources A to D — 16 min. Learner pages 2 and 3. Read Source A aloud; let students read B, C and D. Stop the room once, at the end of Source D, and ask what they notice about the two copies. Take answers; write none of them down; move on.
  4. Period 1 · 2 · Set an Authenticity Test — 8 min. Provisional, and deliberately taken before the method arrives. There is no correct answer and none is keyed. Most classes name the page itself; that is the useful mistake and it is the reason this task comes first.
  5. Period 1 · Read the real-world reference card — 8 min. Learner page 4. Read the two consequences at the foot of the card aloud. The second one is the sentence the rest of the case turns on: no single surface feature proves authenticity or forgery.
  6. Period 2 · 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof — 16 min. The misconception barrier. Do items 1 and 2 together as a class before releasing the rest — both are marked Q, and a class that gets those two right will get the other four. Part B is the paragraph that has to be written before 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record can be trusted.
  7. Period 2 · 4 · Trace Each Copy's Provenance — 12 min. The provenance figure and four short parts. Part D is the one to protect: the honest answer is that the records stop, and a learner who invents where a copy was made has done the opposite of the skill.
  8. Period 2 · 5 · Weigh the Four Evidence Sources — 18 min. Twelve fields; the longest task in the packet. Work the briefing row together, then release. Watch for the class treating all four sources as equally probative — the two participant accounts frame the case, and the log and the custody histories are what authenticate a record.
  9. Period 3 · 6 · Compare the Competing Records — 12 min. Row 1 is given. Say aloud, once, why it is given: because you have to look at the pages, not because looking decides anything.
  10. Period 3 · 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record — 20 min. The graded culminating product. Enforce one rule while circulating: the two evidence links must come from different sources. A memorandum with two links from the same card has not corroborated anything.
  11. Period 3 · 8 · Test What Would Change Your Confidence — 10 min. Transfer. Say once, before they start, that the answer must not mention Copy A or Copy B.
  12. Period 3 · Close and collect — 4 min. Ask for one sentence naming something the class could not settle. Collect the whole packet.

Facilitation prompts

When a class jumps straight to the answer

They will, and they will usually be right about the record and wrong about the reason. Ask: “What would you say to somebody who had put those corrections there on purpose?” That question is the whole case, and it can be asked at any point in any period.

When a class stalls on 5 · Weigh the Four Evidence Sources

Ask what each source would still be true of if the two copies had never existed. Both participant accounts survive that test unchanged; the two records do not. That is the difference between framing a case and authenticating a page.

Transitions and collection points

The three natural stopping points are the end of the reading in period 1, the end of 5 · Weigh the Four Evidence Sources in period 2, and collection at the end of period 3. Nothing needs collecting before the end: 2 · Set an Authenticity Test is provisional and is read rather than marked, and every later task depends on the packet staying with the learner.

The invented and the real, and what to do without the game

Every character, audit, memo copy, custody entry and date here is invented. The archival discipline on the learner card is real, from the two published sources listed on the last page. A conclusion about the two copies proves nothing about the real world, and a real principle is never evidence that the case happened. If a class asks whether the audit really occurred: no — ask back what would have had to survive for anyone to know.

If gameplay is unavailable, nothing changes. The dossier is the assessment record in both routes, no task needs a line that appears only in play, and no rubric criterion refers to the game.

Evidentiary weighting

The four sources are not equally probative, and 5 · Weigh the Four Evidence Sources is where students have to say so. Sources A and B are context: a participant's account of what an audit found, and a participant's account of a pattern across the archive. Both are true inside the case and neither can authenticate a page. Source C is corroborating evidence — an independent record whose own custody is verified, which is the condition on which it can be set against anything else. Source D is direct record evidence: the documents under judgment together with their recorded transmission. A learner who argues Source C into the direct-record role, on the ground that it is itself a verified record, has made a defensible move and the key credits it. A learner who puts A or B into a record role has not.

Reasoning path

The intended route runs: both copies look finished, and one of them is false → looking harder cannot separate them, because the forger is expert → what can separate them is what else agrees with each → Copy B's page and Copy B's custody trail agree with each other, and were made independently → nothing independent agrees with Copy A → Copy B is better supported → and this still does not tell us where either copy was made. Every step is available in the packet. The step students skip is the second one, and 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof is the task that forces it.

The competing records

The case has three positions in it, not two. The first is Copy A, and it is attractive because completeness reads as care. The second is Copy B, which is correct, and which is dangerous because it is easy to reach for the wrong reason. The third position is the one a sharp class finds on its own: that both might be forged and the audit itself is the target. Take it seriously when it appears. The answer is in Source C — the audit's own custody is verified end to end, which is precisely why the log can be used to check anything at all — and a learner who raises it and then resolves it has done better work than one who never thought of it.

Limitations to keep in front of the class

  • Neither trail reaches the making of either copy. The packet cannot say where, when or by whose hand either was written.
  • Copy A's trail being short is not the same as Copy A's trail being complete. Nothing here distinguishes an uneventful history from an unrecorded one.
  • Provenance establishes where a record came from, not that its contents are accurate.
  • The judgment is a judgment about which record is better supported, and it remains open to revision by evidence the packet does not contain. 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record part F is where students say so.

Instructional emphasis

If only one thing survives the week, make it this: the strength of a record is a property of its relationships, not of its appearance. That is what makes 3 · Separate a Clue from Proof the load-bearing task and 7 · Validate the Better-Supported Record the place it gets tested.

Judging whether a document is what it claims to be is a real field. Its name is diplomatics.
