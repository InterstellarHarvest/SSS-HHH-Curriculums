Evidence status — the words the Archive uses
  • documented — recorded, and the record survives
  • reconstructed — the Archive built the scene; no real person or place
  • inferred — worked out by putting sources side by side
  • modeled — drawn or calculated to show how something works
  • debated — scholars disagree, and it is not settled
  • fictional — invented for a case that never happened

The Archive's rule: preservation does not equal historical verification. A record surviving is not the same as a record being checked.

Case 01 The Fertile Crescent THREAD: PRESERVE — knowledge carried forward by saving and replanting seed

Setting Southwest Asia · the scene is set about 9,700 BCE

What changed People cultivated grain that was still the wild kind for centuries. Domestication — a lasting change across the whole plant population — arrived much later, and it arrived cumulatively, over many generations and many regions.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • Charred grain from four excavated sites in northern Syria and southeastern Turkey: 9,844 spikelets examined; 804 identifiable well enough to sort into wild type or domesticated type. In those 804, early deposits are almost entirely wild type and later ones hold more and more non-shattering grain.
  • A site-by-site chronology: people are cultivating wild-type grain from about 9,750 BCE, and non-shattering grain only passes a tenth of the chaff around 8,750–8,250 BCE in southern Syria — and as late as about 6,350 BCE in other regions.

What that evidence supports That the change was cumulative and slow. Repeated harvesting, seed saving and replanting acted as selection, keeping alive a trait that could not survive without people, and the trait spread across generations rather than in a season.

What it does not establish alone It identifies no first person, no first field and no starting date, and the change did not run on one clock. Cultivation is not domestication: people were growing grain on purpose long before the plant population changed.

SOURCE STATUS — Charred grain: documented / observed. The multi-site chronology: inferred. How the change happened: debated. The Archive’s field scene, the woman and her words: reconstructed.

Case 02 Sumer THREAD: RECORD — a record naming a cause the ground does not support

Setting The southern Mesopotamian plain · the scene is set about 2000 BCE

What changed Irrigation without drainage, on a flat plain with a shallow water table, can raise the salt in a field until crops suffer.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The mechanism (FAO): irrigation water carries dissolved salt; a water table within a few metres of the surface delivers salt upward; evaporation removes the water and not the salt; only downward flow with drainage carries salt away.
  • Measured salt tolerance (FAO, after Maas and Grattan 1999): barley keeps full grain yield to 8.0 dS/m and wheat to 6.0 dS/m. These are modern measurements on modern varieties, and both grains are less tolerant while germinating.

What that evidence supports The field-scale salinization mechanism: where water leaves and salt does not, salt accumulates, and a more tolerant grain outlasts a less tolerant one in the same ground.

What it does not establish alone How much soil salt contributed to the wider decline of southern Mesopotamian society is argued among scholars, and this evidence does not settle it. The barley-replacing-wheat reading of the tablets (Jacobsen and Adams 1958) was challenged by Powell (1985), and administrative tablets are not agricultural statistics. Other pressures are argued for the same centuries.

SOURCE STATUS — The mechanism and the tolerance values: documented. The reading of the tablets: debated / uncertain. The soil cross-sections: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the fields and the scribes’ room: reconstructed.

Case 03 County Cork THREAD: RESPOND — what a record of relief does and does not show

Setting Ireland · County Cork · the scene is set in 1845

What changed Phytophthora infestans — late blight — destroyed the potato crop. About a million people died, and the deaths followed from that failure meeting conditions that were already in place.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The same blight in two countries (Ronsijn and Vanhaute): Belgium lost almost 90% of its potato harvest in 1845; Ireland lost about 30% that year and over 75% in 1846. Belgium recorded over 40,000 famine-related deaths and mortality 30–40% above normal; Ireland’s mortality ran at about three times normal and about a million people died.
  • Nicholas Cummins, a magistrate of Cork, travelled to Skibbereen in December 1846 and wrote to the Duke of Wellington about what he found. The letter was published in The Times on 24 December 1846.

What that evidence supports That the pathogen explains the crop failure, and that the size of the crop loss did not set the size of the death toll — Belgium lost far more of its harvest and lost far fewer of its people.

What it does not establish alone It does not rank the conditions that made the difference. Deep dependence among the poorest, land and labour arrangements that left households without money or security, unequal access to the food that remained, and the scale and timing of relief all contributed. Blight alone did not cause the famine, and no single condition — biological or political — was the whole cause.

SOURCE STATUS — The blight science, the comparison and the Cummins letter: documented. The argument about food exports: debated / uncertain. The causes map: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the field, the cottage and the road: reconstructed.

Case 04 Karlsruhe THREAD: TRANSFER — from a bench result to a working plant, and the record of who did what

Setting Karlsruhe and Oppau, Germany

What changed Nitrogen was taken from the air and combined into ammonia — first as a laboratory demonstration, and then, several distinct problems later, as an industrial process.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The 1909 Karlsruhe demonstration, with the apparatus Le Rossignol designed and an osmium catalyst. Published accounts differ on the exact date: Travis (2015) gives 1 July 1909, Appl (1997) gives April 1909. The year is documented; no exact date is settled.
  • The dated sequence that followed: Haber and Le Rossignol file the ammonia patent on 13 August 1909; Mittasch’s screening at BASF finds promoted iron in 1910; Bosch and Lappe answer the hydrogen embrittlement of the steel with a double-walled converter in February 1911; Oppau begins production on 9 September 1913.

What that evidence supports That a laboratory result is not an industrial process. Making it workable took a catalyst that could be made in quantity, steel that survived hydrogen at pressure, and the recycling of unconverted gas.

What it does not establish alone It does not license crediting one person. Haber directed the laboratory programme and introduced the recycle principle; Le Rossignol designed the ammonia apparatus and invented the high-pressure valve; Mittasch found the catalyst; Bosch diagnosed the steel failure and led the works. Laboratory demonstration and industrial engineering are distinct, and both are credited.

SOURCE STATUS — The patent, Travis and Appl: documented. The exact demonstration date: debated / uncertain. The process loop and the tradeoff panel: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the bench: reconstructed.

Case 05 The Dust Bowl THREAD: RESPOND — a response that acted on one cause and not the other

Setting The southern Great Plains of the United States

What changed Soil left the land at regional scale, and a conservation programme followed.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The instrumental drought record: the 1930s drought ran between July 1928 and May 1942; at its peak it covered at least 60% of the Lower 48 States; the average longest unbroken stretch at individual locations was 38.4 months, peaking in March 1935.
  • What the land was already like, and what was done: prairie sod was broken for cropland, leaving bare rootless soil between crops — while about two-thirds of the Great Plains remained in unploughed native vegetation. The Soil Conservation Act was signed on 27 April 1935.

What that evidence supports That the drought (what arrived) and the broken sod (what the land was already like) together produced wind erosion at the scale the region saw. Wind erosion needs loose dry soil and an uncovered surface: neither cause reaches the outcome without the other.

What it does not establish alone Weather alone is wrong, and so is the claim that the weather was irrelevant. The plough alone is wrong too. And the conservation programme spread across the later 1930s while the rains also returned at the end of the decade — nothing in this evidence separates their contributions.

SOURCE STATUS — The drought record, the erosion surveys and the Act: documented. The land-degradation feedback (Cook, Miller and Seager 2009): modeled. How two historians weigh the same two causes: debated. The Archive’s scenes on the plains: reconstructed.

Case 06 The Vertical Farm THREAD: PRESERVE — what a record leaves out becomes what later readers cannot know

Setting A commercial vertical farm, 2041 — fictional

What changed In a sealed indoor growing loop, every machine met the specification it was designed to meet, and the crop died anyway.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • FICTIONAL CASE EVIDENCE The engineered part of the loop met its setpoints throughout. Biological activity in the biofilter held steady, then fell over about 72 hours and ran flat; ammonia rose and nitrate fell; the crops began to die only after that. The company’s public statement blamed a flawed nutrient-cycling design and named an engineer.
  • REAL-WORLD SCIENCE Nitrification is a real microbial process: ammonia and ammonium are oxidised to nitrite and then to nitrate. The organisms are not a fixed pair — ammonia-oxidising archaea as well as bacteria carry out the first step, and comammox Nitrospira can carry out both steps in one organism (Daims et al. 2015; van Kessel et al. 2015). Plants take up inorganic nitrogen mainly as nitrate and ammonium (Hachiya and Sakakibara 2017).

What that evidence supports Within the invented case: that the living part of the loop failed while the engineered part met specification, and that the monitoring watched only the engineered part.

What it does not establish alone Nothing about the real world. Getting the 2041 case right proves nothing about the real world, and a real scientific finding proves nothing about 2041. The invented record also does not establish which logged event collapsed the microbes.

SOURCE STATUS — Two layers, and they never merge. The 2041 facility, its logs, its dates and its public statement are fictional / hypothetical — the Archive invented all of it. The nitrification science and the plant-uptake science are documented real-world findings from real published sources.

What this lesson is

The Campaign 1 Synthesis — not a seventh case. There is no diagnosis to reach and no culprit to name. Students read six evidence recaps of the cases they have already closed and say what the whole campaign shows about how agricultural knowledge is preserved, transferred, tested and revised — and why the record of it matters.

Before you teach

Before class

  • Print the Student Mission (7 pages) and the Accessible Mission (11 pages) as needed.
  • Have students' earlier packets available if you have kept them. They are not required — the recaps are self-contained.
  • Read the misconceptions table on Teacher page 4 before you teach. Two of them will come up out loud.

Optional game launch

  • Campaign 1 Level 7 is the finale. It has no diagnosis and no new historical evidence — it is a closing scene.
  • Play it if you have twelve spare minutes and the class earned an ending. Nothing in this lesson depends on it.
  • The one finale excerpt in the packet is printed on the last learner page and is marked fictional.

The central synthesis — what a strong answer converges on

There is no correct diagnosis here, but there is a centre of gravity. A strong Archive Synthesis says something close to this:

Agricultural knowledge accumulated because people preserved it and passed it on — seed by seed, record by record, patent by plant — and each transfer both extended what people could do and created something new that could go wrong. What stayed constant across twelve thousand years is that the record is always narrower than the event: every case in Campaign 1 has a boundary its evidence cannot cross. That is why the qualifications are worth preserving alongside the findings.

Roughly 60 minutes

  1. 10 min — 1 · Read the Campaign Record and 2 · Trace the Long Yield: read the cards, build the rail.
  2. 15 min — 3 · Compare Continuity and Change: the paired comparison.
  3. 15 min — 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism: where a correct mechanism stops.
  4. 15 min — 5 · Write the Archive Synthesis: the synthesis.
  5. 5 min — 6 · What Should the Archive Preserve?: the exit.

Essential campaign evidence

Six recap cards, each carrying its own certified evidence, its own limit, and its own source status. Nothing in this packet is new. Every figure and date is carried forward from the released Cases 01–06.

The likely sticking point

4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism. Students who have done six investigations have been rewarded for finding the mechanism. This task asks them to hold a mechanism they know is right and say what it still cannot explain. Expect the first attempt to be a restatement of the mechanism. The contrast strip on the Accessible page and the Case 04 worked example are the fastest way in.

A line to open with

“For six cases you have been the person who works out what happened. Today you are the person who decides what gets kept — and what a reader a hundred years from now will be able to work out from what you left them.”

What to collect

3 · Compare Continuity and Change, 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism, 5 · Write the Archive Synthesis and 6 · What Should the Archive Preserve?. 1 · Read the Campaign Record is reference and is not keyed. 2 · Trace the Long Yield is short and is best checked on the spot.

If the technology fails

Nothing breaks. The packet is complete on paper and no task requires a screen. If you had planned to launch Level 7, read the finale excerpt on the last learner page aloud instead — it is the same text.

Lesson overview

Campaign 1 followed agricultural knowledge across roughly twelve thousand years: repeated seed-saving in southwest Asia; an irrigation practice that salted its own fields; a pathogen that met a dependent population; a laboratory result that took five years and four kinds of work to become a factory; a drought that met broken sod; and, in an invented future case, a sealed loop whose machinery worked perfectly while its living part died. This unit puts those six side by side and asks what the whole run shows.

Guiding question

How does agricultural knowledge change as people preserve, transfer, test, and revise it—and why does the historical record matter?

Learning objectives

Complete teaching procedure

Every Student task appears below in order. Times are the 60-minute core route; the optional extensions are marked and are not part of the assessed contract.

  1. Open — 3 min. Put the guiding question on the board and say plainly that there is no case to solve today. Tell them the six cards are reference, not reading comprehension, and that nothing is being tested from memory. This matters: a class that thinks it is being quizzed on six old cases will read defensively.
  2. 1 · Read the Campaign Record — 5 min. Students read the six recap cards. Direct them explicitly to the line on each card that says what the evidence does not establish alone; that line is what Tasks 4, 5 and 6 are built on. Do not collect anything. This task is reference and is not keyed.
  3. 2 · Trace the Long Yield — 5 min. Students use the chronology rail to name one continuity and one change, each with the cases it rests on. Point out the note above the rail: the six spans are not measured the same way, and Case 01's is a set of overlapping evidence windows rather than one continuous event. Check these on the spot — a student who cannot find a continuity will struggle at Task 5.

    Facilitation prompt: “A continuity is not ‘farming happened in all six.’ What specific thing kept happening?”

  4. 3 · Compare Continuity and Change — 15 min. Students pick one case from Cases 01–03 and one from Cases 04–06 and complete the ten-field organizer. The early/late split is required and is what makes the comparison cross real time. Circulate during the last two rows: the evidence row and the qualification row are where the task is won or lost, and both are drawn from the cards rather than from memory.

    Facilitation prompt: “Which line on the card tells you what that evidence cannot do?”

  5. 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism — 15 min. Students choose two of Cases 02, 03, 05 and 06 and take each through local mechanism → what it explains → what it does not explain alone → additional context needed. Teach the shape before they start. Use the Case 04 worked contrast printed in the Accessible edition — a mechanism that is entirely correct and still stops short — and say aloud that none of the four mechanisms on the list is a mistake. Cases 01 and 04 are deliberately not offered; if a student asks why, the answer is on Teacher page 5.

    Facilitation prompt: “Your mechanism is right. Now: what happened that it does not account for?”

  6. 5 · Write the Archive Synthesis — 15 min. The culminating product. Students write roughly two compact paragraphs carrying a continuity, a change, evidence from at least two cases, at least one qualification, and why the record matters. This is not Claim–Evidence–Reasoning and students should not be told to write a claim. It is an explanation with five load-bearing parts. Sentence count is not scored; the five requirements are.

    Transition: collect nothing yet — Task 6 is on the same route and takes five minutes.

  7. 6 · What Should the Archive Preserve? — 5 min. The exit. Students choose one case and say what a later reader could misunderstand if one critical piece of evidence, qualification or source relationship vanished. Read the finale excerpt above it aloud if you did not play Level 7 — then say that the Archive's ending is a story, and this question is the one the story does not ask.

    Collection point: collect the packet here.

  8. Optional extension — not assessed. Ask which single case most changed what later societies could know, rather than what they could do. This is a genuinely open question and makes a good discussion close; it is not part of the graded contract and no rubric criterion covers it.

If you are short of time, the defensible shortened route is to drop 3 · Compare Continuity and Change and keep Tasks 2, 4, 5 and 6. This is a modified assessment route: you are no longer assessing the paired continuity/change comparison, and rubric criterion 1 loses its main evidence. Do not shorten by dropping the qualification requirement in 5 · Write the Archive Synthesis — that is the unit's spine.

Grouping

3 · Compare Continuity and Change and 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism both benefit from two minutes of talk before writing, because both ask for a judgment a student can test out loud. Pairs beat fours here: with four, one student picks the cases and the rest copy.

5 · Write the Archive Synthesis is written alone. It is the individual product.

Early finishers

Send them to a third case in 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism — four are offered and they owe two. It costs nothing to mark and it is the task most worth repeating.

Do not start them on 5 · Write the Archive Synthesis early. The synthesis goes better after the class has talked.

Formative checks

Likely misconceptions

What students sayRedirect
“It is in the archive, so it is true.”Archived does not mean verified. Preservation and verification are two different things — Archive Orientation's rule. A record surviving tells you someone kept it, not that anyone checked it. Case 06's public statement survived and was wrong.
“Each case is better than the one before — change always means progress.”Ask them to place Case 02 and Case 05 on that line. Irrigation was an advance that salted the fields; breaking the sod was an advance that let the soil leave. Change is not the same as improvement, and the campaign contains both directions.
“We found the mechanism, so we have explained the event.”The whole of 4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism. Late blight is the correct mechanism and Belgium lost more of its harvest than Ireland did while losing far fewer of its people. One correct mechanism does not explain the whole historical outcome.
“Case 02 and Case 05 are basically the same case.”They are analogous in shape and different in cause. Salt accumulates because water leaves and salt does not; soil leaves because wind acts on bare dry ground. Analogous cases do not have identical causes, and the comparison is only worth making if the difference survives it.
“So in 2041 a vertical farm collapsed.”No. The 2041 facility is fictional. The Archive invented the farm, the company, the engineer and every date. The nitrification science in that card is real and published; the farm is not. A fictional case is not documented future history, and getting the 2041 case right proves nothing about the real world.
Source-status boundaries to hold

Reconstructed scenes are not testimony. Modeled figures are not measurements. Debated readings are not settled findings. A fictional case is not history. If a student's synthesis rests on any of these as though it were documented, that is the correction to make — it is rubric criterion 4.

Continuity and change — the pathways students find

Defensible continuities

  • People keep selecting, saving and passing on what works (01 → 04 → 06).
  • Someone always writes it down, and the record is always narrower than the event (02, 04, 05, 06).
  • Every intervention creates a new dependency (02, 04, 06).
  • The evidence always stops short of the whole outcome (02, 03, 05, 06).

Defensible changes

  • The scale of intervention: one field, then a plain, then the nitrogen supply of a continent (01 → 02 → 04).
  • The speed: thousands of years for a trait, five for a factory (01 → 04).
  • Who holds the knowledge: a household, then a temple, then a firm (01 → 02 → 04/06).
  • What gets monitored — and what therefore can be noticed (05 → 06).

Preservation and transfer

The campaign's own chain: seed kept back and resown (01) → a practice recorded on tablets that later scholars read and re-read (02) → a crop and a dependency transferred into a whole population (03) → a laboratory result transferred into an industrial plant through four distinct kinds of work (04) → a practice adopted at scale, then revised by policy (05) → a system whose record preserved only the half that was being watched (06).

Why Cases 01 and 04 are not offered. Case 01's reasoning is cumulative change across generations rather than a mechanism falling short of an outcome, and Case 04's is attribution across distinct kinds of work. Forcing either into this frame for symmetry would teach a false shape. Case 04 is used instead as the worked contrast, precisely because it cannot be chosen.

Case 06 — the two-layer truth

Layer 1 is fictional. The 2041 facility, the company, the engineer, the logs, the dates and the public statement were invented by the Archive. They may be reasoned about; they may never be cited as facts about the real world. Layer 2 is real published science. Nitrification, the diversity of nitrifying organisms and plant uptake of nitrate and ammonium are documented findings from real sources. The non-merger rule: getting the 2041 case right proves nothing about the real world, and a real scientific finding proves nothing about 2041.

Using the two rubrics together

The quick rubric is for the room: it is what you scan while students are still writing, and it tells you who to stand next to. The analytic rubric is for the pile — used once, afterwards, on the collected packet. Do not score the quick rubric. Two instruments scoring the same work produce two grades and one argument.

Read criterion 4 first. It is the only criterion carrying floors, and a packet that trips a floor rarely rewards close reading on the other four until that is addressed.

A worked judgment

A student writes: “Farming kept changing. In Sumer the salt ruined the fields and in the Dust Bowl the soil blew away because they ploughed it. Both of them wrecked their land. That is why we keep records, so it does not happen again.”

Where that lands. Criterion 1: 2 — a change is named, a continuity is not, and “farming kept changing” is a truism. Criterion 2: 2 — two cases are named with nothing specific attached to either. Criterion 3: 2 — the record appears as a moral rather than as something that governs what can be known. Criterion 4: 1 — “because they ploughed it” makes land use the whole cause of the Dust Bowl, which trips the floor. Criterion 5: 2.

What to say to that student. Not “add more detail.” Say: the drought is on your card, and it is a cause. Put it back in — then tell me what the two causes together did that neither could do alone.

How to use this key

Four tasks are keyed: 3, 4, 5 and 6, with 2 checked formatively. Every keyed field below carries a completed exemplar, not a description of one. Where students choose their own cases, one complete model pathway is given in full and the defensible alternatives follow it in short form. No section here defers to variation in place of a model. Task 1 is reference and is not keyed.

A · One meaningful continuity — model. Across all six cases people keep hold of what worked and hand it on to whoever comes next — the seed set aside in Case 01, the practice written onto tablets in Case 02, the patent and the plant design in Case 04. The way it is passed on changes completely; that it is passed on at all does not.

B · One meaningful change — model. The scale each intervention works at. In Case 01 the unit is one household's field over generations; by Case 04 a single process at Oppau is supplying nitrogen to whole national agricultures within five years of a bench demonstration. What one decision could reach grew enormously.

Also creditable. Continuities: someone always writes it down, and the record is always narrower than the event (02, 04, 05, 06); every intervention creates a new dependency (02, 04, 06); the evidence always stops short of the whole outcome (02, 03, 05, 06). Changes: the speed of change — thousands of years for a trait in Case 01, five for a factory in Case 04; who holds the knowledge, from household to temple to firm; what gets monitored and therefore what can be noticed (05 → 06).

Not creditable, because it is true of any six agricultural cases and rests on none of them: “people grew food in all of them”, “farming continued”, “the weather mattered every time”. A continuity must be specific and must name cases.

Floor. A change described as steady improvement — that farming simply got better each time — does not earn credit at B. Case 02's irrigation and Case 05's ploughing were both advances, and both are why those cases exist.

Common wrong turns at Task 2.

  • A continuity that is really a category. “All six are about farming” is what the campaign is made of, not something it shows. Ask what specific practice or condition recurred.
  • A change with only one end of it. “Technology improved” names no before and no after. A creditable change says what it was in one case and what it had become in another.
  • Cases named without a reason. “Cases 01 and 06” with nothing attached is a citation, not a judgment.

3 · Compare Continuity and Change — continued

Specific supporting evidence — model. Case 02: the documented mechanism has evaporation removing the water and not the salt, and the measured tolerances put barley at 8.0 dS/m against wheat at 6.0 dS/m, so a shift toward barley is what a salting field would produce. Case 05: the drought ran from July 1928 to May 1942 and covered at least 60% of the Lower 48 at its peak, while about two-thirds of the Great Plains remained in unploughed native vegetation — the same weather, and the sod held where it had not been broken.

An important qualification — model. Neither case supports the large claim it invites. Case 02's mechanism is a field-scale mechanism: how much soil salt contributed to the wider decline of southern Mesopotamian society is argued among scholars and this evidence does not settle it, and the barley-replacing-wheat reading of the tablets was itself challenged. In Case 05 the conservation programme spread across the later 1930s while the rains also returned at the end of the decade, and nothing in the evidence separates their contributions.

The comparison itself, if a student draws it out: both cases are an improvement that changed the ground it stood on, and in both the record of the outcome is easier to read than the cause of it. Credit but do not require this.

Defensible alternative pathways — short form. Each is a complete route, not a hint.

  • 01 with 04. Knowledge: choosing which seed to keep, against fixing nitrogen from the air. Preserved/transferred: the seed and the habit of saving it, against a patent, a catalyst recipe and a plant design. Changed: the plant population itself across two to four thousand years, against the nitrogen supply of whole agricultures within five years. Consequence: grain that cannot sow itself without people, against fertiliser at industrial scale. Evidence: the 804 identifiable spikelets and the dated windows from about 9,750 BCE; the 1909 demonstration, the 1910 catalyst, the February 1911 converter, Oppau from 9 September 1913. Qualification: Case 01 names no first person, field or date, and cultivation is not domestication; Case 04's exact demonstration date is debated and no single person may be credited with the industrial process.
  • 03 with 06. Knowledge: depending on one crop, against depending on one automated loop. Preserved/transferred: a dependency, in both. Changed: what a single failure could take down. Consequence: about a million deaths in Ireland; a total crop loss in the invented facility. Evidence: Belgium lost almost 90% of its harvest and recorded mortality 30–40% above normal while Ireland lost about 30% then over 75% and ran at about three times normal mortality; in Case 06 the engineered systems met every setpoint while the biological activity fell over about 72 hours. Qualification: Case 06 is fictional and cannot be evidence about the real world, and Case 03's evidence shows more than one condition contributed without ranking them.
  • 02 with 04. Both are interventions in a nutrient supply, one by moving water and one by making nitrogen. Preserved: the practice and the record in each. Changed: what could be grown, and where. Qualification: Case 02's region-scale claim is unsettled; Case 04 requires both the laboratory result and the industrial engineering to be credited.
  • 01 with 05. Both turn on what the ground does when people work it repeatedly. Evidence: the dated windows against the drought record and the two-thirds unploughed figure. Qualification: Case 01 fixes no date and Case 05's two causes cannot be separated from each other.

Floors. Two cases from the same group is not a completed task — the early/late split is the structure. A qualification row that hedges without naming what the evidence cannot do earns no credit. In any pairing that uses Case 06, treating the 2041 facility as something that happened caps criterion 4 at 1.

Common wrong turns at Task 3.

  • Two summaries instead of a comparison. Both columns filled correctly, with nothing in the response that holds them against each other. The evidence row is where the comparison has to become visible.
  • A consequence that repeats the change. If what changed says “the soil blew away” and consequence says “the soil was lost”, only one row has been answered.
  • A qualification about the student rather than the evidence. “I am not sure” is not an evidence limit. The limit belongs to the record, and every card prints one.
  • Preserved and transferred treated as the same word. Case 01 preserved a seed and Case 04 transferred a process; both rows can be answered, and they are not answered the same way.

Two complete model pathways follow — Case 03 and Case 06. Any two of Cases 02, 03, 05 and 06 are permitted; the other two are modelled on the next page. Cases 01 and 04 are not offered and a response using either is incomplete rather than wrong.

Model pathway — Case 03 · County Cork

  1. Local mechanism. Phytophthora infestans infects the potato plant, spreads through the living tissue and rots the tubers; a crop of one variety in cool damp weather goes down fast.
  2. What it explains. Why the potato harvest failed, why it failed so quickly, and why it failed again in 1846 after a partial failure in 1845 — about 30% of the harvest lost that first year and over 75% the next.
  3. What it does not explain alone. The death toll. Belgium lost almost 90% of its potato harvest in 1845 — far more than Ireland lost that year — and recorded mortality 30–40% above normal, while Ireland's ran at about three times normal and about a million people died. The same organism, a larger crop loss, and an outcome an order of magnitude apart. The mechanism cannot be what made the difference.
  4. Additional context or evidence needed. How deeply the poorest depended on the potato and how unevenly; how land and labour were held, and whether a household had money or security once its own crop was gone; who could reach the food that remained; and what relief was provided, at what scale, and when. Case 03's evidence establishes that more than one of these contributed — it does not rank them.

Model pathway — Case 06 · The Vertical Farm Both stages of this pathway stay inside the invented case. Nothing in it is evidence about the real world.

  1. Local mechanism. In the invented 2041 facility the living part of the loop stopped converting ammonia while the dosing machinery kept delivering nutrient on schedule; ammonia rose, nitrate fell, and the crops died after that.
  2. What it explains. Why a crop could starve and burn at the same time while every machine met its specification — the conversion step is the one part of the loop no pump performs.
  3. What it does not explain alone. Why nobody noticed, and why the public record named the wrong cause. The mechanism says what failed; it does not explain why the monitoring watched only the engineered zone, why three events lethal to a microbial community were filed as within tolerance, or why the company's statement blamed a flawed design and named an engineer. It also does not establish which logged event collapsed the microbes — three candidates sit within three days and nothing separates them.
  4. Additional context or evidence needed. What the monitoring was specified to measure and who decided that; how the living subsystem was commissioned and verified compared with the steel; and what investigation, if any, stood behind the public statement. And a standing limit: this is an invented case, so no amount of additional evidence inside it would establish anything about the real world.

How to score the two pathways. They are scored together, not averaged. A learner who finds a genuine limit in one case and restates the mechanism in the other has shown the reasoning once — that is criterion 4 at a 3, not a 2, provided the one they found is real. What does not reach a 3 is two restatements, however fluent.

Common wrong turns at Task 4.

  • The limit is the mechanism again, negated. “It does not explain what it does not cause.” Ask: name the thing that happened that this does not reach.
  • The mechanism is declared wrong. It is not. Every one of the four is correct, and a learner who rejects it has answered a different question.
  • Additional context becomes a wish. “More research” is not an answer; “land-use records for the same counties as the drought record” is.

4 · Test the Limits of a Correct Mechanism — continued

Model pathway — Case 02 · Sumer

  1. Local mechanism. Irrigation water carries dissolved salt onto a flat plain with a shallow water table; evaporation removes the water and leaves the salt; without drainage there is no downward flow to carry it away.
  2. What it explains. Why soil in an irrigated field without an outfall becomes saltier over time, and why the more tolerant grain outlasts the less tolerant one in the same ground — barley holding full yield to 8.0 dS/m against wheat's 6.0.
  3. What it does not explain alone. The wider decline of southern Mesopotamian society. That is argued among scholars and this evidence does not settle it. The barley-replacing-wheat reading of the tablets was itself challenged, administrative tablets are not agricultural statistics, and other pressures are argued for the same centuries.
  4. Additional context or evidence needed. Measurements of soil salinity from dated ancient contexts rather than modern ones; an independent reading of the tablets; and some way of weighing the other pressures against this one. None of that is in the packet, which is why the region-scale claim stays open.

Model pathway — Case 05 · The Dust Bowl

  1. Local mechanism. Wind moves loose dry soil that has no cover on it, by saltation, surface creep and suspension.
  2. What it explains. How the soil physically left the fields, and why it left those fields — the mechanism needs dry loose soil and an uncovered surface, and both were present where the sod had been broken.
  3. What it does not explain alone. Why it happened in that decade and that region rather than generally. Wind erosion is always available; the drought of July 1928 to May 1942 supplied the dryness and the plough-up supplied the bare surface, and about two-thirds of the Plains that stayed in native vegetation did not blow. Neither cause reaches the outcome alone. Nor does the mechanism explain why the dust stopped: the conservation programme and the returning rains cannot be separated on this evidence.
  4. Additional context or evidence needed. Land-use records showing what was broken and when; the drought record set against them; and something that separates the effect of conservation practice from the effect of the returning rains — which this packet does not have.

Floors for this task. A does not explain alone box that restates the mechanism is not a completed field. Writing that the drought was not a cause, or that the plough alone caused the Dust Bowl, is refused in both directions. Presenting the 2041 facility as documented history caps criterion 4 at 1.

Complete model synthesis. Roughly the length and depth expected at a 3–4. It uses Case 02 and Case 05, matching the Task 3 model; a synthesis built on any two or more cases is equally acceptable.

Across twelve thousand years the one thing that never changes is that people keep what works and hand it on — the seed heads set aside in the Fertile Crescent, the practice recorded on Sumerian tablets, the patent and the plant design at Karlsruhe. What changes is the scale of what a single decision can reach. In Case 02 an irrigation practice with no outfall salted the ground it fed, one plain at a time; by Case 04 one process at Oppau was supplying nitrogen to whole national agricultures within five years of a bench demonstration.

The evidence for this is specific and it is also bounded. The salinization mechanism is documented — evaporation removes the water and not the salt — and the measured tolerances put barley at 8.0 dS/m against wheat's 6.0, which is why a salting plain would show a shift toward barley. In Case 05 the drought ran from July 1928 to May 1942 and covered at least 60% of the Lower 48, while about two-thirds of the Great Plains stayed in unploughed native vegetation and did not blow. But neither case settles the large question it raises: how much soil salt contributed to Sumer's wider decline is still argued, and the conservation programme and the returning rains cannot be separated from one another on this evidence. That is why the record matters, and why its qualifications matter as much as its findings — the Archive can tell us what a field did far more reliably than it can tell us why a society ended, and a later reader who kept only the findings would end up confident and wrong.

5 · Write the Archive Synthesis — continued

Checking the five requirements against the model. Continuity — people keep and hand on what works. Change — the scale a single decision reaches. Evidence from at least two cases — the salinization mechanism and the two tolerance values from Case 02; the drought dates, the 60% figure and the two-thirds unploughed figure from Case 05. Qualification — the region-scale claim is unsettled and the two candidate causes of the dust ending cannot be separated. Why the record matters — the final sentence.

Accessible parity. The same five requirements govern the Accessible edition, where they may be met in bullet points. A bulleted response that carries all five is a 3 or a 4 on criterion 5; bullets are a permitted response mode and are never penalised.

Floors. No qualification anywhere caps criterion 4 at 1. Blight alone caused the famine, the plough alone caused the Dust Bowl, the drought was not a cause, salinity caused the fall of Sumer, Haber alone created the industrial process, cultivation is domestication — each of these is a refused claim and each caps criterion 4 at 1. Treating the fictional 2041 facility as documented history does the same.

Model — Case 03 · County Cork. The piece that goes missing: the European comparison, which records that Belgium lost almost 90% of its potato harvest in 1845 and recorded mortality 30–40% above normal while Ireland lost about 30% that year and ran at about three times normal mortality. Without it, a later reader would have the pathogen and the death toll and nothing standing between them, and would conclude that late blight caused the famine — a clean, confident, wrong account in which no human arrangement had to be examined at all. The comparison is what makes the mechanism visibly insufficient.

Model — Case 06 · The Vertical Farm. The piece that goes missing: the source status itself — the line saying that the 2041 facility is fictional. The evidence would survive and read exactly like a case report, and a later reader would take an invented failure for a documented one and cite it as what happens when a real automated farm is built. Preservation would have worked perfectly and verification would have failed completely, which is the Archive's own rule stated backwards.

Defensible alternatives — short form. Case 01: lose the count of 804 identifiable spikelets out of 9,844 examined, and the trend claim looks like it rests on nearly ten thousand pieces instead of eight hundred — a far stronger finding than the evidence supports. Or lose the dated windows, and cultivation and domestication collapse into one event. Case 02: lose Powell's challenge to the tablet reading, and a contested interpretation reads as a settled finding. Case 04: lose the record of Le Rossignol's, Mittasch's and Bosch's work, and one man appears to have invented an industrial process single-handed. Case 05: lose the two-thirds-unploughed figure, and the disaster looks like something that happened to the whole region rather than to the part of it that had been broken; or lose the returning rains, and conservation policy appears to have ended the dust by itself.

Floors. Naming a piece of evidence without saying what a later reader would then get wrong is half the task. “We would not know what happened” is not a misunderstanding — the task asks for a specific wrong belief the surviving record would support.

What a 2 looks like, for contrast. Set against the model above, this is the response that most often arrives instead:

“Agriculture changed a lot over time. In Sumer irrigation made the soil salty and in the Dust Bowl the ploughing made the soil blow away. People learned from their mistakes and made new rules like the Soil Conservation Act. Records matter because otherwise we would forget what happened.”

Two cases are named and both are broadly accurate, so this is not a 1. But there is no continuity, no specific evidence — no figure, date or source — and no qualification, and the closing sentence treats the record as a reminder rather than as the thing that governs what can be known. Criteria 1, 2, 3 and 4 all sit at 2. The fastest route to a 3 is not more writing: it is one number from a card and one sentence saying what the evidence cannot settle.

Case 01 The Fertile Crescent THREAD: PRESERVE — knowledge carried forward by saving and replanting seed

Setting Southwest Asia · the scene is set about 9,700 BCE

What changed People cultivated grain that was still the wild kind for centuries. Domestication — a lasting change across the whole plant population — arrived much later, and it arrived cumulatively, over many generations and many regions.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • Charred grain from four excavated sites in northern Syria and southeastern Turkey: 9,844 spikelets examined; 804 identifiable well enough to sort into wild type or domesticated type. In those 804, early deposits are almost entirely wild type and later ones hold more and more non-shattering grain.
  • A site-by-site chronology: people are cultivating wild-type grain from about 9,750 BCE, and non-shattering grain only passes a tenth of the chaff around 8,750–8,250 BCE in southern Syria — and as late as about 6,350 BCE in other regions.

What that evidence supports That the change was cumulative and slow. Repeated harvesting, seed saving and replanting acted as selection, keeping alive a trait that could not survive without people, and the trait spread across generations rather than in a season.

What it does not establish alone It identifies no first person, no first field and no starting date, and the change did not run on one clock. Cultivation is not domestication: people were growing grain on purpose long before the plant population changed.

SOURCE STATUS — Charred grain: documented / observed. The multi-site chronology: inferred. How the change happened: debated. The Archive’s field scene, the woman and her words: reconstructed.

Case 02 Sumer THREAD: RECORD — a record naming a cause the ground does not support

Setting The southern Mesopotamian plain · the scene is set about 2000 BCE

What changed Irrigation without drainage, on a flat plain with a shallow water table, can raise the salt in a field until crops suffer.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The mechanism (FAO): irrigation water carries dissolved salt; a water table within a few metres of the surface delivers salt upward; evaporation removes the water and not the salt; only downward flow with drainage carries salt away.
  • Measured salt tolerance (FAO, after Maas and Grattan 1999): barley keeps full grain yield to 8.0 dS/m and wheat to 6.0 dS/m. These are modern measurements on modern varieties, and both grains are less tolerant while germinating.

What that evidence supports The field-scale salinization mechanism: where water leaves and salt does not, salt accumulates, and a more tolerant grain outlasts a less tolerant one in the same ground.

What it does not establish alone How much soil salt contributed to the wider decline of southern Mesopotamian society is argued among scholars, and this evidence does not settle it. The barley-replacing-wheat reading of the tablets (Jacobsen and Adams 1958) was challenged by Powell (1985), and administrative tablets are not agricultural statistics. Other pressures are argued for the same centuries.

SOURCE STATUS — The mechanism and the tolerance values: documented. The reading of the tablets: debated / uncertain. The soil cross-sections: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the fields and the scribes’ room: reconstructed.

Case 03 County Cork THREAD: RESPOND — what a record of relief does and does not show

Setting Ireland · County Cork · the scene is set in 1845

What changed Phytophthora infestans — late blight — destroyed the potato crop. About a million people died, and the deaths followed from that failure meeting conditions that were already in place.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The same blight in two countries (Ronsijn and Vanhaute): Belgium lost almost 90% of its potato harvest in 1845; Ireland lost about 30% that year and over 75% in 1846. Belgium recorded over 40,000 famine-related deaths and mortality 30–40% above normal; Ireland’s mortality ran at about three times normal and about a million people died.
  • Nicholas Cummins, a magistrate of Cork, travelled to Skibbereen in December 1846 and wrote to the Duke of Wellington about what he found. The letter was published in The Times on 24 December 1846.

What that evidence supports That the pathogen explains the crop failure, and that the size of the crop loss did not set the size of the death toll — Belgium lost far more of its harvest and lost far fewer of its people.

What it does not establish alone It does not rank the conditions that made the difference. Deep dependence among the poorest, land and labour arrangements that left households without money or security, unequal access to the food that remained, and the scale and timing of relief all contributed. Blight alone did not cause the famine, and no single condition — biological or political — was the whole cause.

SOURCE STATUS — The blight science, the comparison and the Cummins letter: documented. The argument about food exports: debated / uncertain. The causes map: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the field, the cottage and the road: reconstructed.

Case 04 Karlsruhe THREAD: TRANSFER — from a bench result to a working plant, and the record of who did what

Setting Karlsruhe and Oppau, Germany

What changed Nitrogen was taken from the air and combined into ammonia — first as a laboratory demonstration, and then, several distinct problems later, as an industrial process.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The 1909 Karlsruhe demonstration, with the apparatus Le Rossignol designed and an osmium catalyst. Published accounts differ on the exact date: Travis (2015) gives 1 July 1909, Appl (1997) gives April 1909. The year is documented; no exact date is settled.
  • The dated sequence that followed: Haber and Le Rossignol file the ammonia patent on 13 August 1909; Mittasch’s screening at BASF finds promoted iron in 1910; Bosch and Lappe answer the hydrogen embrittlement of the steel with a double-walled converter in February 1911; Oppau begins production on 9 September 1913.

What that evidence supports That a laboratory result is not an industrial process. Making it workable took a catalyst that could be made in quantity, steel that survived hydrogen at pressure, and the recycling of unconverted gas.

What it does not establish alone It does not license crediting one person. Haber directed the laboratory programme and introduced the recycle principle; Le Rossignol designed the ammonia apparatus and invented the high-pressure valve; Mittasch found the catalyst; Bosch diagnosed the steel failure and led the works. Laboratory demonstration and industrial engineering are distinct, and both are credited.

SOURCE STATUS — The patent, Travis and Appl: documented. The exact demonstration date: debated / uncertain. The process loop and the tradeoff panel: modeled. The Archive’s scenes at the bench: reconstructed.

Case 05 The Dust Bowl THREAD: RESPOND — a response that acted on one cause and not the other

Setting The southern Great Plains of the United States

What changed Soil left the land at regional scale, and a conservation programme followed.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • The instrumental drought record: the 1930s drought ran between July 1928 and May 1942; at its peak it covered at least 60% of the Lower 48 States; the average longest unbroken stretch at individual locations was 38.4 months, peaking in March 1935.
  • What the land was already like, and what was done: prairie sod was broken for cropland, leaving bare rootless soil between crops — while about two-thirds of the Great Plains remained in unploughed native vegetation. The Soil Conservation Act was signed on 27 April 1935.

What that evidence supports That the drought (what arrived) and the broken sod (what the land was already like) together produced wind erosion at the scale the region saw. Wind erosion needs loose dry soil and an uncovered surface: neither cause reaches the outcome without the other.

What it does not establish alone Weather alone is wrong, and so is the claim that the weather was irrelevant. The plough alone is wrong too. And the conservation programme spread across the later 1930s while the rains also returned at the end of the decade — nothing in this evidence separates their contributions.

SOURCE STATUS — The drought record, the erosion surveys and the Act: documented. The land-degradation feedback (Cook, Miller and Seager 2009): modeled. How two historians weigh the same two causes: debated. The Archive’s scenes on the plains: reconstructed.

Case 06 The Vertical Farm THREAD: PRESERVE — what a record leaves out becomes what later readers cannot know

Setting A commercial vertical farm, 2041 — fictional

What changed In a sealed indoor growing loop, every machine met the specification it was designed to meet, and the crop died anyway.

Evidence the campaign certified

  • FICTIONAL CASE EVIDENCE The engineered part of the loop met its setpoints throughout. Biological activity in the biofilter held steady, then fell over about 72 hours and ran flat; ammonia rose and nitrate fell; the crops began to die only after that. The company’s public statement blamed a flawed nutrient-cycling design and named an engineer.
  • REAL-WORLD SCIENCE Nitrification is a real microbial process: ammonia and ammonium are oxidised to nitrite and then to nitrate. The organisms are not a fixed pair — ammonia-oxidising archaea as well as bacteria carry out the first step, and comammox Nitrospira can carry out both steps in one organism (Daims et al. 2015; van Kessel et al. 2015). Plants take up inorganic nitrogen mainly as nitrate and ammonium (Hachiya and Sakakibara 2017).

What that evidence supports Within the invented case: that the living part of the loop failed while the engineered part met specification, and that the monitoring watched only the engineered part.

What it does not establish alone Nothing about the real world. Getting the 2041 case right proves nothing about the real world, and a real scientific finding proves nothing about 2041. The invented record also does not establish which logged event collapsed the microbes.

SOURCE STATUS — Two layers, and they never merge. The 2041 facility, its logs, its dates and its public statement are fictional / hypothetical — the Archive invented all of it. The nitrification science and the plant-uptake science are documented real-world findings from real published sources.
