A · The farm, and the floor it died on STATUS: fictional / hypothetical · the invented 2041 case

What the building was. Ten storeys of crops grown with no soil and no sunlight, in a sealed loop where the same water and the same nutrients circulate round and round. It was built to prove a city could grow its own food. It ran for one full cycle and most of a second.

What the floor looks like now. The lights are on. The pumps are running. The water is moving. Every tray on every floor is dead.

What the crops show. Two things at once, which should not go together:

  • Nitrogen starvation — the pale, stunted growth of a plant that cannot get enough nitrogen.
  • Caustic burn at the roots — chemical scorching, of the kind caused by too much of something.

What the full system scan reports. Pumps, dosing units, climate control, circulation, sensors: every mechanical system operational. No faults. The feed system never stopped delivering nutrient — not for an hour.

Hold that contradiction. The plants starved while being fed, and burned at the same time. Nothing in this card explains how both can be true. Task 4 does.

B · The engineering record STATUS: fictional / hypothetical · the invented 2041 case

The facility logged everything its machines did. The record for the failure period, and the engineer's own account of it:

  • Nutrient metered to the gram, on schedule, every hour.
  • Pumps at pressure throughout.
  • Temperature held at setpoint.
  • Every setpoint the design specified was met, to the final hour.
  • No alarm was raised at any point.

The engineer's reading of his own logs: “We built beautiful hardware around a living community, and then we only ever watched the hardware.”

Ask yourself what a machine log is able to record. It records what the machine did, and what the machine was built to measure. It cannot report a condition nobody gave it an instrument for — so silence in this record is not the same as good news. You will write that down properly in Task 6.

C · The biological trace STATUS: fictional / hypothetical · the invented 2041 case

One instrument in the building watched the living part of the loop — the microbes in the biofilter. This is what it recorded.

  • Biological activity in the biofilter held steady for 58 days, as it had since the system was commissioned.
  • Over about 72 hours it fell, and then ran flat.
  • From that point on: ammonia rose. Nitrate fell.
  • The crops began to die only after that.

This card records a sequence, not a cause. It says the microbes' activity collapsed. It does not say why, it names no organism, and there is no second biological record in this case to check it against.

The science file · part 1 · the chemistry and the organisms

You are now crossing into Layer 2. Everything from here to Source J is real, published, and cited. None of it is about the 2041 farm. It is how a loop like that one would have to work.

F · Ammonia, ammonium, and what nitrification does STATUS: documented · real · US government science agency

From the United States Environmental Protection Agency (the EPA), in its CADDIS scientific reference on ammonia:

  • “Ammonia nitrogen” is two substances, not one. It includes the ionized form, ammonium (NH4+), and the unionized form, ammonia (NH3).
  • pH decides the balance between them. The EPA states that an increase in pH favours formation of the more toxic unionized form (NH3), while a decrease favours the ionized form (NH4+). Temperature affects toxicity too.
  • Nitrification is defined there as the oxidation of ammonia to nitrite (NO2−) and nitrate (NO3−) — a job done by bacteria and other microbes, not by any machine.

Read the limit of this source carefully. The EPA wrote it about rivers and lakes and the animals in them — its toxicity statements are about aquatic animals, particularly fish, and gill membranes. It gives you the forms and the pH rule. It gives you no number for any crop plant, and this packet does not invent one.

G · The discovery that broke the two-species rule STATUS: documented · real · two peer-reviewed papers

For decades, textbooks taught nitrification as a relay between two kinds of microbe: one does ammonia → nitrite, a different one does nitrite → nitrate. Nobody had ever seen one organism do both. Microbiologists found the split genuinely puzzling.

Then, in 2015, two research groups published in the same issue of Nature, independently, the same discovery:

  • Daims and colleagues, and van Kessel and colleagues, both found Nitrospira bacteria carrying all the genes needed to do the whole conversion alone — ammonia all the way to nitrate, in one organism.
  • They named it comammox, short for complete ammonia oxidation.
  • Their analysis indicated comammox organisms occur in a range of different environments.

Be precise about what this did and did not do. It did not replace one universal rule with another — comammox is not now “the” answer everywhere. What these two papers retired is the word always.

H · Who was actually doing the work in a real biofilter STATUS: documented · real · peer-reviewed measurement of a working system

Bartelme, McLellan and Newton (2017) did something nobody in the 2041 case ever did: they went and measured which organisms were living in a real, running biofilter — the sand biofilter of a commercial freshwater fish farm.

  • They note that people who design these systems typically name the main nitrifying organisms as Nitrosomonas (for the first step) and Nitrobacter (for the second).
  • What was actually there was not that. Ammonia-oxidising archaea — a different domain of life from bacteria altogether — dominated, at roughly six hundred thousand times the abundance of Nitrosomonas.
  • Comammox Nitrospira carried the most abundant ammonia-oxidising gene in the system.
  • That nitrifying group stayed stable over time, even while the rest of the bacteria in the filter shifted with normal day-to-day operations.

One biofilter, one system, one study — it cannot tell you what lives in any other filter. What it does establish is that the organisms a designer expects and the organisms actually doing the work can be completely different. Task 7 asks you to use this.

The science file · part 2 · the plants

I · What forms of nitrogen a plant can actually use STATUS: documented · real · peer-reviewed review

Hachiya and Sakakibara (2017), reviewing how plants take up nitrogen:

  • Plants take up inorganic nitrogen mainly as nitrate and ammonium — both forms, through separate transporters in the root.
  • A mixture of the two is reported to be better for growth than either one on its own.
  • Nitrate is the main form in most well-aerated soils; ammonium is more common in acidic or waterlogged ones.

This card exists to stop a wrong sentence. It is easy to finish this case believing that nitrate is the one form a plant has any use for. It is not — the review says plainly that plants take up both. What went wrong in the 2041 case has to be said more carefully than that, and Task 4 makes you say it.

J · How much is too much? STATUS: documented · real · peer-reviewed review

Esteban, Ariz, Cruz and Moran (2016), reviewing ammonium toxicity in plants:

  • Ammonium stress affects virtually every plant species — but how badly varies enormously.
  • The review reports wide variation between species and within them: some species and genotypes actually prefer ammonium; others are extremely sensitive to it.
  • The threshold at which ammonium becomes toxic depends on the species, the ecotype, even the cultivar, and on the growing conditions.

This is why there is no number anywhere in this packet. There is no single level of ammonium that is “the toxic one” for plants, so none is printed. If someone gives you one, ask them: for which plant, at what pH?

Launch sheet

This case runs over three ordinary class periods. It is the campaign's fictional case, and it is the only one in Campaign 1 where the events did not happen. That is not a weakness to apologise for — it is the instrument. Because nothing in the case file is real, a learner cannot fall back on what they already know, and the only way through is to audit the records in front of them.

Before class

Print the twelve-page Student Mission or the seventeen-page Accessible Mission. Both carry the whole dossier and every figure, so either packet is completely teachable with no device in the room. Read the two-layer truth notice on learner page 1 yourself first, and plan to read it aloud. It is the single thing this case cannot be taught without.

Central case resolution

A sealed growing loop recycles its own nitrogen. Waste ammonium and ammonia are converted by microbes into nitrite and then nitrate; the crops take nitrogen back up. In the case, that microbial conversion stopped. Nitrogen kept arriving, the machinery kept dosing to specification, and the plants starved and burned at once because the nitrogen was there in the wrong form. A biological subsystem failed inside an engineering success — and the monitoring only ever watched the engineering.

Essential evidence

One pairing carries this case, and it is a pairing rather than a single record. The engineering log says the machines were fine. The biological trace says something living stopped days before anything else moved. Neither one is enough on its own — a clean machine log by itself is only a machine log, and a biological collapse by itself might have been caused by a broken pump. Put them together and a hardware failure is excluded and a sequence appears. 6 · Audit the Records Part B is built so that the learner has to name that pair and say why one alone will not do.

Likely sticking point

The overcorrection at the end. A class that discovers the engineer did not do it will very often land on “so it was nobody's fault.” That is the opposite error and it is just as wrong. 8 · Audit the Public Record Part C exists for exactly this: the learner has to name an institutional question that is still open and cite the record that raised it.

What to collect

Collect the whole packet at the end of period 3. 9 · Explain the Failure is the graded culminating product; its Part A requires source letters and layer labels, and its Part D is the transfer check, answered about a water-treatment plant that no dossier card mentions.

Teacher framing line

“Here is a building where every single machine worked perfectly, right through to the end, and everything alive inside it died. Nobody is lying to you in this case. Every record you're about to read is honest. The problem is that the record with the most authority — the one with the numbers and the timestamps — was measuring the wrong half of the building. By Friday you'll be able to say what failed, and why the official answer got it wrong, and here's the hard part: you'll have to do that without ending up at ‘so it was nobody's fault.’”

Classroom and technical fallback

If devices fail mid-period, switch to the no-game route and change nothing else. If you lose a period, protect 3 · Draw the System Boundary, 6 · Audit the Records, 8 · Audit the Public Record and 9 · Explain the Failure; 1 · Build the Case Vocabulary can be finished as homework. Dropping 3 · Draw the System Boundary is the one cut that breaks the lesson — without the boundary a learner has no way to hold “the machines were fine” and “the system failed” in the same head. If you must cut it, that is a modified assessment route and rubric criterion 1 can no longer be scored honestly.

Success criteria

What this case must carry from the audit. Two boundaries. They are not disclaimers to be read once; they shape the figures, the tasks, the key and the rubric.

One — the two layers must never merge. Sources A to E are invented. Sources F to J are real published science. The whole case is designed so a learner can be right about 2041 without having learned anything false about the world, and can be right about nitrification without believing a vertical farm collapsed in 2041. If a student asks whether this really happened, the answer is a flat no — and then the better question: does that change whether the reasoning was any good?

Fiction, science, and source boundaries

  • Nothing in the case file happened. The facility, the company, the engineer, the spokesperson, the mayor, the schoolchildren, the day numbers, the seventy-two hours, the fifty-eight days — every one of them was written for this case. F · Ammonia, ammonium, and nitrification STATUS: documented · real · US government science agency

From the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA):

  • “Ammonia nitrogen” is two substances, not one:
    • ammonium, NH4+ — the charged form;
    • ammonia, NH3 — the uncharged form.
  • pH decides the balance. The EPA says: pH up → more of the more toxic form, ammonia (NH3). pH down → more ammonium (NH4+). Temperature matters too.
  • Nitrification is the oxidation of ammonia to nitrite (NO2−) and nitrate (NO3−) — done by bacteria and other microbes. Not by any machine.

Read the limit of this source. The EPA wrote it about rivers and lakes and the animals in them — fish, and gill membranes. It gives you the forms and the pH rule. It gives you no number for any crop plant.

G · The discovery that broke the two-species rule STATUS: documented · real · two peer-reviewed papers

The old rule: textbooks taught nitrification as a relay between two kinds of microbe. One does ammonia → nitrite. A different one does nitrite → nitrate. Nobody had ever seen one organism do both.

Then in 2015, two research groups published in the same issue of Nature, independently, the same discovery:

  • Daims and colleagues, and van Kessel and colleagues, both found Nitrospira bacteria with all the genes to do the whole job alone.
  • They named it comammox — short for complete ammonia oxidation.
  • Comammox organisms occur in a range of different environments.

Be careful what this means. It did not swap one universal rule for another. What these papers retired is the word always.

H · Who was actually doing the work STATUS: documented · real · peer-reviewed measurement of a working system

Bartelme, McLellan and Newton (2017) did something nobody in the 2041 case ever did. They went and measured which organisms were actually living in a real, running biofilter — the sand biofilter of a commercial fish farm.

What designers expect to be there:

  • Nitrosomonas — for the first step.
  • Nitrobacter — for the second step.

What was actually there:

  • Ammonia-oxidising archaea — a completely different domain of life from bacteria — dominated.
  • They were about 600,000 times as abundant as Nitrosomonas.
  • Comammox Nitrospira carried the most abundant ammonia-oxidising gene.
  • That nitrifying group stayed stable over time, even while other bacteria came and went.

One biofilter, one study. It cannot tell you what lives in any other filter. What it does show is this: the organisms a designer expects and the organisms actually doing the work can be completely different. You will use this in Task 7.

J · How much is too much? STATUS: documented · real · peer-reviewed review

Esteban, Ariz, Cruz and Moran (2016), reviewing ammonium toxicity in plants:

  • Ammonium stress affects nearly every plant species.
  • But how badly varies enormously — between species, and even between cultivars of the same crop.

  • The threshold depends on the species, the ecotype, the cultivar, and the growing conditions.

This is why there is no number anywhere in this packet. There is no single level of ammonium that is “the toxic one” for plants. If someone gives you one, ask: for which plant, at what pH?
