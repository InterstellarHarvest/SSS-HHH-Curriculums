# Balanced Page Fill and Vertical Rhythm v1.0.2

**Status:** Approved shared clarification  
**Scope:** Student, Accessible, Teacher, Answer Key, and derived Grayscale pages  
**Authority:** Human design judgment remains final

## Purpose

Use meaningful surplus vertical space to improve task separation, readability, and writing usability when a page otherwise leaves substantial unused space while major tasks remain visually compressed. The goal is balanced rhythm, not a requirement to fill every page.

## Required order of use

When meaningful vertical space remains, apply it in this order:

1. Add modest separation between major numbered tasks so one task does not visually run into the next.
2. Enlarge response, process-model, diagram, table-writing, or notes areas only when the expected response genuinely benefits from additional room.
3. Add modest internal padding where it improves readability or writing use.
4. Preserve a reasonable intentional reserve at the bottom of the page.

## Response-area rules

- Do not enlarge a field intended for one word, a short phrase, or a single line.
- Keep classification, status, label, criterion, constraint, and similar compact fields compact.
- A multi-line field may grow modestly when students are expected to write several lines, a substantial sentence, a paragraph, an explanation, CER content, or a detailed design response.
- Growth must remain proportional to the expected answer. A field intended for about three to five lines must not expand to occupy half a page merely because it is the only flexible component.
- Do not turn every eligible field into a large writing box. Judge the prompt, expected response length, page composition, and classroom usability.

## Prohibited fill tactics

Do not increase typography, enlarge headers or identity elements, stretch short-answer fields, distribute surplus equally among every component, create exaggerated gaps between related prompts, or force a page to appear completely full.

## Validation rule

Validation should flag a page only when meaningful unused vertical space coexists with visibly compressed major-task spacing or an undersized multi-line work area. It must not flag intentional blank space, compact short-answer fields, or a response area already proportionate to its expected answer length. Automated measurements are diagnostic signals; human design review is the final authority.
